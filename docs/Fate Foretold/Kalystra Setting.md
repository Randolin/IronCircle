A LOCATION FOR DAGGERHEART

KALYSTRA

OVERVIEW

Designed by Aaron Holloway

Themes: Sun-Bleached, Merciless, Celebratory

Tier: 1

Concept: A cliff-ringed island city where gladiators bleed for coin, gods, and omens written in shifting stars.

DISTINCTIONS

Kalystra is a hard island with soft lies. The sea is bright enough to sting your eyes, the stone is pale enough to glare back, and the city of Heliakros rises from the port as if it was built to block the horizon on purpose. Everyone here talks about honor. Everyone here keeps a ledger.

WHITE CLIFF CIRCLE
Heliakros is held in by a ring of white cliffs, carved by quarry hands, sea caves, and old prayers. The cliffs do not merely surround the city; they define it. From the water, Kalystra looks protected. From inside, it looks caged. There are stairways cut into the stone for workers, pilgrims, and the unlucky. Most are unmarked on public maps. Most are still used.

You might find:
• A narrow shrine carved into the cliff face, packed with cheap offerings and expensive secrets.
• A quarry crew working in silence, as if words would draw attention from above.
• A seabird nesting in a broken helm, pecking at bronze like it tastes salt.

THE CROWN OF ZEPHYRON
Formally a tribute to Zephyron, the sky-sovereign of Kalystra, the Crown is the arena that rules the week. Its upper tiers are clean marble and painted sigils. Its lower tiers are damp stone, iron rings, and routines that grind people down to something sellable. The city insists the games are tradition. The crowd insists the games are fun. The priests insist the gods are watching. All three can be true at the same time.

You might find:
• A worker repainting a safety sigil for the third time, swearing the paint keeps crawling out of place.
• A bronze placard listing combatants and odds, as casual as a weather report.
• A corridor below the sands where the air tastes metallic, like a coin held too long in the mouth.

CONSTELLATION FEVER
The Agon Astron always comes near the solstice, but this year the stars have become a public argument. Priests point at the sky like accountants accusing each other of fraud. Sailors refuse certain routes. Gamblers adjust their odds by watching the wind. Everyone has a theory. None of them are quiet about it.

You might find:
• Two rival star-priests arguing over the same line of light, both insisting the other is committing blasphemy.
• A vendor selling "blessed" water in stoppered amphorae, sourced from a fountain nobody can name.
• A sudden stillness in the breeze, followed by a gust that arrives like it was late on purpose.

WELL-WORN PATHWAYS
Heliakros is built in layers: quay, markets, neighborhoods, then temples and council halls. Paths are old and polished by feet. Stairways are steep and unforgiving. Some routes are public for commerce; others are unofficial for survival. Anyone who lives here can tell, just by the sound of steps, whether you are lost.

You might find:
• Dockhands walking with their eyes up while newcomers keep their eyes down to avoid being cheated.
• Chalk marks on a stair landing that are half directions, half warnings.
• A tired guard who knows every shortcut, because their job depends on arriving before the trouble does.

UNDERROOT
Beneath Heliakros are cisterns, old quarry tunnels, sealed sanctums, and the working guts of the Crown. Official maps ignore Underroot. The people who need it do not. Entrances are hidden behind pantry walls, under loose paving stones, inside storage shrines, or through maintenance doors that "nobody has a key for" until someone does.

You might find:
• A chain-gang hallway lit by sputtering lamps, with numbers scratched into the stone for every day survived.
• A brackish cistern where coins have been thrown for luck, then never retrieved.
• A narrow passage that smells like sea salt and incense, leading somewhere that should not have a door.

GM PRINCIPLES

Make the city beautiful in ways that do not care about you.
Heliakros is polished where it wants to be seen and brutal where it does its work. Let both exist in the same street.

Show how devotion becomes infrastructure.
Faith here is not private. It is carved, painted, enforced, and monetized. The gods are present, but so are their bureaucrats.

Treat spectacle like a weapon.
The arena is not just an event. It is leverage. It changes what people will forgive, what they will pay for, and what they will pretend not to notice.

LANDMARKS

THE CROWN OF ZEPHYRON
Number of Public Entrances: 12
Number of Doors That Matter: Fewer than 12
The Crown is marble and thunder above, iron and breath below. The sands are raked smooth each morning, as if a perfect surface could erase what happened yesterday. In the underlevels, the stone remembers anyway.

THE SANDS
The combat floor is a shallow basin ringed by sigil-lines and drain slats. The city claims this is to keep the arena safe. The staff claims it is to keep it clean. The gamblers claim it is to keep the blood from clumping the sand.

THE SIGIL GALLERY
A corridor of painted walls and plaster reliefs celebrating past victors. Names are recorded when the sponsors approve. Some figures are faceless where history was inconvenient.

THE CHAIN CELLS
The lowest chambers are simple: iron rings set into stone, abrasive cots, a slat of light near the ceiling, and just enough room to hate yourself in. Guards call it "holding." Prisoners call it "practice."

THE WHITE CLIFF SWITCHBACKS
Longest Stair Run Without a Landing: 178 steps
Most Common Injury: Pride
These stairs are carved directly into the cliff ring and connect the quay to the upper city. Officially, they are for work crews and temple deliveries. Unofficially, they are for anyone who needs to move without being seen from the main roads. There are alcoves along the way where offerings are left, mostly to stop the cliff from "noticing" you.

THE OLD QUARRY FACE
Stonecutters still work this rock, but many parts are abandoned. Chisel marks from centuries ago look fresh at sunrise, when the light hits the grooves and makes them appear wet.

THE SEA WINDOWS
Narrow slits in the cliff, cut to vent caverns and cisterns. Locals say the sea "breathes" through them. Guards say they are structural. Prisoners say they smell like freedom.

AETHERION STEPS
Number of Temples in View: More than feels necessary
Wind Pattern: Unreliable on purpose
A long rise of stone leading to the temple terraces and council halls. On solstice week, processions climb these steps at dawn and descend at dusk, carrying banners, censers, and whatever story the city needs told today. At the top, the sea looks close enough to touch and far enough to mock you.

THE BRONZE ASTROLABE
A public instrument mounted on a plinth, maintained by the augurs and constantly argued over by everyone else. Children spin it for luck. Priests threaten to arrest them. Nobody follows through because even the strictest officials like luck.

SETTLEMENTS

HELIAKROS
City Leaders: Archon Pelagon, Praetor Halcyon
Known for: The Agon Astron and the Crown of Zephyron.
Heliakros is a port city that behaves like a capital when it wants something. It feeds on visitors, on pilgrims, on fighters, on spectacle. During the Agon Astron, it becomes louder, richer, and more dishonest, then calls the dishonesty "tradition."

THE LOW QUAY
Where ships unload, where sailors argue, where bribes feel like tips. This is where Nyra's crew can be found most days, and where Tamon Goldhand's runners place quiet bets that become loud later.

THE CLIFFMARKET
A tiered bazaar built into the lower cliffs. Shops are stacked like shelves, and so are the people. The best food is here. The fastest scams are also here.

TEMPLE ROW
A tight line of shrines and small temples where priests compete for foot traffic like merchants. Many offerings are sincere. Many are performed. Nobody can always tell which is which.

THE CHAINWARD
A neighborhood that pretends it is not defined by the Crown. It is where workers, guards, trainers, and those who "used to fight" live. The Chainward has the highest concentration of cheap taverns and quiet knives in the city.

THE COUNCIL TERRACES
White stone, wide stairs, banners that always look new. This is where the city becomes official and where Eudoros keeps records that can ruin a person without spilling a drop of blood.

GULL'S REST
Village Leaders: Lysene Hearthmother (unofficial), the dock elders (official)
Known for: Nursing the shipwrecked and refusing to ask where they came from.
A small settlement on the lee side of the island, built for those who do not want to be in Heliakros but cannot afford to leave Kalystra. Many people call it a fishing village. It is more accurate to call it a waiting room.

THE SALT SHRINE
A crude altar built from stacked stones and driftwood. Sailors leave offerings here before risky crossings. Half the offerings are sincere. Half are bribes to the universe.

THE NETLOFTS
A maze of drying racks and repair stalls. It smells like fish, rope, and arguments that never resolve.

QUARRY OF MARCHING STONE
Foreman: Marro (when he is not needed below the Crown)
Known for: Stonework, silent labor, and doors that appear overnight.
A working quarry settlement whose people are paid well and watched closely. The quarry face has old tunnels that connect to Underroot. The city says they are sealed. The quarry workers smile when asked.

FACTIONS

THE CROWN CHAIN
Most Commonly Sighted: Under the arena, at the gates, in the Chainward.
Misconception: They are all cruel.
The Crown Chain is the bureaucracy of violence: wardens, trainers, scribes, cleaners, and guards who keep the Agon Astron functioning. Some are sadists. Some are tired. Many are both, depending on the hour. The Chain is not a monolith, but it has one shared belief: the games must continue.

THE KEYRING LAW
Keys are status. Access is power. A guard with a heavy keyring is either trusted or bluffing. Most are bluffing. The smart ones bluff well.

THE SAND BOOKS
Doros keeps records: contracts, punishments, bribes disguised as fees, and favors disguised as misunderstandings. The Sand Books are not public, but their consequences are.

THE CLEAN SIGILS
The sigil painters are treated like essential workers and scapegoats in equal measure. When a charm fails, they are blamed. When it works, the priests take credit.

PRINCIPLES
The Crown Chain does not publish its rules; it enforces them.
• "Order is mercy. Disorder is work."
• A clean arena is a safe arena, for someone.
• Never admit the Crown needs you. It always does.

THE SUNCOURT COUNCIL
Most Commonly Sighted: Council Terraces, temple events, sponsor boxes at the Crown.
Misconception: They are unified.
The Suncourt Council is Heliakros acting official. They govern trade, festivals, public order, and the city's reputation among the isles. They are also a rotating argument between families, patrons, and priests. During Agon week, they present a clean face. Underneath, they count votes like knives.

THE ARCHON'S MASK
Archon Pelagon speaks as if the city itself is talking. His opponents do not call him a liar. They call him "ceremonial," which is the local way to say "dangerous."

THE PRAETOR'S HAND
Praetor Halcyon oversees enforcement and security. Her guards are polite until they are not. She prefers problems that can be solved by rules and dislikes anything that requires imagination.

THE REGISTRAR'S INK
Eudoros keeps records of arrivals, permits, debts, and "misunderstandings." Many citizens fear him more than they fear the gods.

PRINCIPLES
The Council runs on rituals and favors.
• "Public order is private profit."
• Promise nothing you cannot enforce.
• When in doubt, schedule a procession.

THE AETHERION AUGURS
Most Commonly Sighted: Temple Row, Aetherion Steps, rooftops at dusk.
Misconception: They all agree on what they see.
The Augurs read wind and star, but also read people. They are priests, astronomers, and performers in equal measure. They believe the gods are real because the gods behave like real power: inconsistent, petty, and expensive. This week, the sky has become a public dispute, and the Augurs are bleeding credibility by the hour.

THE BRONZE ASTROLABE
The public instrument is both sacred tool and civic toy. When it shifts unexpectedly, the Augurs blame humidity, then blame each other.

THE ORACLE'S CHAMBER
High Oracle Seryth delivers pronouncements that turn into policy. Many augurs resent this. Many citizens worship it. Seryth treats both reactions as weather.

THE SIBILANT SCHOOL
A small order of diviners known for learning by rote and reciting like knives. Outsiders call them fanatics. They call themselves prepared.

PRINCIPLES
The Augurs worship patterns and survive politics.
• "Interpretation is power."
• A sign is not truth. It is leverage.
• Never read the same sky twice.

THE HEARTH LEAGUE
Most Commonly Sighted: Cliffmarket, Chainward, Gull's Rest.
Misconception: They are harmless.
The Hearth League is not official. It is neighbors, cooks, medics, dockhands, and former fighters who refuse to let the week consume everyone without consequence. They keep people fed, hide those who need hiding, and mend what the city breaks. They also remember everything.

THE HEARTH HALL
A communal room behind a bakery where soup is always on and questions are optional. It is the closest thing Heliakros has to mercy that is not transactional.

THE OLD FIGHTERS
Some victors stayed. Some losers survived. They do not agree on much, but they agree the Crown takes more than it gives.

THE QUIET ROOMS
Back rooms above shops where people can sleep without being asked for papers. The League does not advertise them.

PRINCIPLES
The Hearth League survives by staying small and useful.
• "Feed them first. Ask later."
• Never argue in public when you can act in private.
• If the city will not protect its people, the people will.

THE LAUGHING VEIL
Most Commonly Sighted: Festival streets, sponsor events, the victory celebration.
Misconception: They are just entertainers.
The Laughing Veil is a traveling knot of performers, stagehands, and "special guests" who move through Heliakros like smoke through open doors. Some are honest artists. Some are criminals with good timing. A few are something worse. During Agon week, everyone wants their laughter. The Veil is happy to sell it.

THE RIBALD BLESSING
A crude toast that gets repeated until it becomes tradition. Once everyone knows it, nobody can remember who taught it to them.

THE MASK BOXES
Storage trunks that hold costumes, props, and things that are not props. Guards rarely search them. Nobody wants to be the guard who ruins the party.

THE LAST PERFORMANCE
A private show after the finale for sponsors and officials. Invitations are status. Attendance is leverage.

PRINCIPLES
The Veil lives by applause and access.
• "If they laugh, they forgive."
• Never arrive first. Never leave last.
• A good mask is a key without metal.

ENVIRONMENTS

The Crown Underlevels - Tier 1 Exploration
Description: Cellblocks, maintenance corridors, cisterns, and the working bones beneath the arena sands.
Impulses: Confine the Weak, Hide the Useful, Reward the Cruel
Potential Adversaries: Crown Chain Guard, Salt Wight, Arena Hound (use Crown Chain Guard stats with a Bite reskin)
Difficulty: 12
FEATURES
Hidden Door - Passive: Finding a viable way into Underroot requires a Progress Countdown (3). Applicable approaches include Instinct (spot patterns), Knowledge (recall layouts), Finesse (manual searching), or Presence (getting a worker to talk).
How are entrances disguised? What does the Crown assume nobody would dare to move?

Chain-Gang Rush - Passive: If you want to abstract a messy scuffle, call for a Group Action. On a success with Fear, each PC marks Stress and either 1 HP or 1 armor slot. On a failure, each PC marks 2 Stress and some combination of 2 HP and/or armor slots.
Who joins the rush: prisoners, guards, or both? What does the noise draw?

Keyed Gates - Action: Reveal a gate drops or locks at the worst moment. The party can pick it (Finesse), force it (Strength), or find the bypass (Knowledge/Instinct).
What does the gate protect: supplies, a ritual room, or a secret ledger?

Alarm - Reaction: On a failure or success with Fear, the Chain posts more patrols. For the rest of the scene, attempts to move unseen have disadvantage until the party creates a distraction or defeats the active patrol.
How does the alert manifest: torchlight, whistles, or the sound of keys running?

Solstice Procession - Tier 1 Traversal
Description: Packed streets, banners, incense, and bodies moving uphill toward Aetherion Steps as if the city is being pulled by a rope.
Impulses: Separate Allies, Offer Temptations, Turn Movement into Theater
Potential Adversaries: Pickpockets (use Crown Chain Guard stats but lower ATK by 1), Bronze Harpy, Zealots (use Crown Chain Guard stats with Presence-based intimidation)
Difficulty: 11
FEATURES
Crushing Crowd - Passive: Moving quickly through the procession requires a Progress Countdown (4). Applicable approaches include Finesse (slip through), Presence (lead people), Instinct (anticipate gaps), or Strength (push through without starting a riot).
What does the crowd consider a sacred line nobody may cross?

Rooftop Route - Passive: A faster path exists above the street. Reaching it requires a successful climb or negotiation (Strength/Finesse/Presence). On success with Fear, the party gains speed but draws attention.
Who notices first: Halcyon's guards, the Veil, or the Augurs?

Dropped Offering - Action: A brazier tips, amphorae shatter, or a banner pole falls. The party can avoid (Instinct), catch (Strength), or redirect (Finesse). On a failure, someone important gets hurt or blamed.
Who gets blamed publicly, and who gets blamed privately?

Divine Attention - Reaction: On a failure or success with Fear, the procession shifts around a PC as if making room for them. It feels like honor. It also feels like being singled out.
Which temple notices, and what do its priests decide that means?
