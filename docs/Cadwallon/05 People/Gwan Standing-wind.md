# Gwan Standing-wind

> "We took this fief from the Markropets. Their grandchildren are richer than ours now. *Tell me how that happened.*"
> — Gwan, in private session with his senior advisors

Peer of [[Trophy]]. The third Standing-wind to hold the peerage since **Bran Standing-wind** received the fief from the Duke in 932 (after the **expropriation of the Markropets**). **The house is in decline.** Trophy's commercial enterprises are slowly being eclipsed by the [[Var-Nokkt]] axis; the Markropets — who lost the fief — have, through generations of careful financial rebuilding, **become richer than the family that displaced them**. Gwan may be the last Peer of his line.

## What He Looks Like

A human Barhan in his late fifties, **graying but still imposing**. Heavy-boned, slow to move, fast to speak. Wears the Standing-wind colors — green and bronze — in working tunics; ceremonial robes only when protocol demands. **Eyes that look tired.** Smile that does not quite reach.

## What He's Inherited

- A fief that was given to his grandfather as a *reward* and is now treated as a *liability*
- A noble-house treasury that has been slowly bleeding for two generations
- A standing humiliation: the **Markropet family's prosperity** is publicly known and quietly mocked at upper-city dinners
- Trade routes that other families control better
- A reputation for being *the family the Duke is least likely to call on*

## What He's Trying To Do

- **Restore Standing-wind commercial relevance** — through a series of speculative trade deals, several risky
- **Marry a Standing-wind into a stronger house** — his two adult children are publicly available for negotiation
- **Survive long enough** to hand his son something other than ruins

## The Markropet Question

The displaced **Markropet family** never accepted the 932 expropriation. They have spent **three generations** rebuilding their fortune through commerce, marriage, and quiet investment. They are now visibly richer than the Standing-winds. **They have not formally challenged the fief's transfer.** They do not need to. They are winning the long game.

> [!secret] What the Markropets actually want
> The Markropets are *not* trying to reclaim the Trophy fief. They are trying to **buy out every Standing-wind asset adjacent to it** — warehouses, trade contracts, residences in surrounding districts — so that when the Standing-wind line eventually fails or sells out, the Markropets are the only buyers left. **They are positioning for the next century, not this one.** Gwan suspects but cannot prove this.

## Possible Loss of the Peerage

If the Standing-wind decline continues at its current rate, the Duke may have grounds to **revoke the peerage** under emergency provisions — something not done since the 932 expropriation itself. This is the worst-case scenario for Gwan and the central nightmare of his late nights.

## Relations

- **[[Duke Den Azhir]]** — formally cordial. The Duke watches Trophy carefully. Gwan does not know whether the Duke is preparing to support him or replace him.
- **Markropets** — historical enemies. They do not openly engage. They simply *keep being right*.
- **[[Var-Nokkt]]** — eclipsing rival in commerce
- **[[Drakaër]] family** — sympathetic; the Drakaër family is one of the few who treat Gwan as an equal
- **[[Guild of Goldsmiths]]** — financiers; they have called Standing-wind debts politely twice this year
