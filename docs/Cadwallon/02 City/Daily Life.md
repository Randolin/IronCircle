# Daily Life

> "Even before considering oneself as an elf, a goblin, or a Wolfen, every Cadwë is above all a citizen of Cadwallon."

What it actually feels like to live in the Free City — the people, the tongue, the money, the calendar of fears and festivals, and the strange shared identity that binds a hundred warring cultures into one citizenry.

## Who's Here

About **200,000 "true" Cadwës** (natives) — roughly two-thirds of the population. The rest are:

- **Foreigners** — merchants, envoys, sea-dogs passing through on business; little influence on daily life
- **Refugees** — fleeing the [[The Rag'narok|Rag'narok]], arriving penniless after expensive, miserable journeys. The lucky integrate via guild work; the unlucky end up homeless, or as alchemists' test subjects, or as [[Guild of Usurers|Usurer]] body-collateral.
- **Established immigrants** — settled into guilds and neighborhoods, slowly becoming Cadwë

## Being a Cadwë

To be a Cadwë is to be **born in Cadwallon and raised in its tradition of freedom**. Cadwë identity is deliberately abstract — it overrides (without erasing) one's ancestral culture. A Cadwë dwarf is still a dwarf, but he is a *Cadwë* first.

The values: the endless quest for riches, the will to settle and prosper, pride in being "part of the advancement of a society detached from the nations and their wars." Pride in being Cadwë is less about scorning refugees than about **genuinely believing in the project** — a free city that refuses the Rag'narok.

Paths to belonging: contribute to a guild's prosperity (and your own), enter a noble house's service (and learn to be a courtier), or — for the impatient and idealistic — join the [[Militia]] or the [[Free Leagues]].

## The Cadwë Language

A living potpourri that "perfectly reflects the nature of the City of Thieves." Built on the **jargon of the [[Dogs of War]]** plus **[[Akkylannie|Akkylannian]] grammar** (easy to learn), then layered with:

- **Barhan, Akkylannian, and Syhar** — the dominant influences
- **Dwarven (Gheim)** — most technical terms
- **Goblin** — navigation terms
- **Ogre** — the insults (the most commonly used and widely preferred, naturally)

It's rich in neologisms, multilingual references, and original sayings, under perpetual construction, and spreading across [[Aarklash]]. The **upper-city dialect** is more structured and conservative, based on the four embassy languages — and sprinkled with impressive [[Cynwäll]] terms.

## Money

The **klû** — originally the coin of the goblins of [[No-Dan-Kar]] — has become the international standard. Locally the **ducat** (D) is minted by the [[Guild of Goldsmiths|Goldsmiths]]. The city's real motto is "My kingdom for a ducat!" — and everything, legal or not, has a price.

## The Calendar

The [[Guild of Cartomancers|Cartomancers]] maintain the **Free Calendar**: 21 "months" of 19 days each, plus a leap day (dropped every fourth year). The week is three six-day spans — the Prime, the Median, the Final — and days have no names ("the fifth day of the Median of Decarde"). *(Full detail in [[Calendar]].)*

### The Seasons
- **Time of Banners** (spring) — the [[Guild of Blades|Blade]] companies raise oriflammes; the merchant fleet prepares to sail; jubilant atmosphere in [[Kraken Harbor]]
- **Season of Blades** (summer) — the bloodiest campaign season; risk of siege; the Duke patrols the borders, the [[Guild of Blacksmiths|blacksmiths]] work double
- **Time of Return** (autumn) — fleets and caravans come home laden; also when unsatisfied mercenaries prowl and Akkyshan raids loom
- **Season of Whispers** (winter) — intrigue, alliance-building, and the feared [[Akkyshan]] raids on the outlying fiefs during the long nights

> [!note] A strict prohibition
> Since Vanius's rule, it is **forbidden to celebrate the equinoxes and solstices** in Cadwallon. The reason is officially unknown. *(GM: the true solstice/equinox points are when the [[Labyrinth]] brushes closest to the surface and [[Desire]]'s dreams run strongest. Vanius banned the celebrations to keep crowds from gathering at the wrong places at the wrong times.)*

## The Festivals

- **Week of Freedom** (2nd–7th of Odecime) — the great carnival of independence; six days of masks, music, and license. The annual free assembly meets the day before.
- **Week of the Kraken** (14th–19th of Quint) — the merchant fleet sets sail; celebration in the harbor; everyone but the [[Guild of Ferrymen|Ferrymen]] gets time off
- **The Ducal Jubilee** (15th of Octose) — celebrates [[Duke Den Azhir|Den Azhir]]'s accession (996); the **only day lower-city citizens may enter the upper city**, in militia-supervised processions; free leaguers renew their allegiance
- **Day of Lanterns** (10th of Ondre) — commemorates the Battle of Kaïber; children play at war, adults hang lanterns; Darkness-aligned refugees go into hiding (sometimes violently)
- **Day of Ashes** (15th of Hexadime) — Cadwallon becomes a dead city; ashes scattered to convince the dead the city is a ghost town; the taboo is **never look anyone in the eye** — everyone walks hooded, heads bowed. *(This is the day the [[Coiling Emissaries]] arrive each year since 999.)*
- **Day of Flowering** (12th of Privime) — celebrates the first [[Magical Groves|magical groves]] after the Dogs of War's "deforestation"; the orcs observe it most carefully, with shaka ceremonies in the groves

## The Shadow Over Everything: Death

The first **tide of the dead** — when the lake carried a putrid stench and hundreds of corpses, poisoning the water and driving the inhabitants into the jungle — left a permanent mark. Cadwës carry an intense fear of death and what happens to the body. **Most corpses are burned** to avoid "polluting" the ground; the great funeral parlor (the Last Stop, at the Smoky Wisps) sits well away from dwellings at the lake's edge.

The tide still strikes occasionally, less violently than before. Heavy rain is the warning. People lock their doors, stock water, and scrub their homes with boiling water until it passes.
