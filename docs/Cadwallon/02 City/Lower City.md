# Lower City

> "Most of its denizens know only the hideous features of the lower city. The choice between hard labor or extreme poverty, a life devoted to the guilds' service or the unpredictability of day-to-day struggle."

The sprawling, ground-level majority of Cadwallon — the part that developed **outside the walls**, at the feet of the [[Dyrsin]] Tower. Eight of the eleven fiefs are here. This is where most Cadwës are born, work, struggle, and die. It is poorer, denser, more dangerous, and far more alive than the [[Upper City]] above it.

## What It Is

- **Developed outside the citadel walls** — unplanned, organic, sprawling across the peninsula at the base of the Tower
- **Eight fiefs**: [[Soma]], [[The Automaton]], [[The Rampart]], [[Kraken]], [[Ogrokh]], [[Trophy]], [[Gamehead]], [[Ghieronburg]]
- **Where the harbors are** — [[Kraken Harbor]], the [[Port of the Ondine]], [[Buoyancy Bay]], the working docks
- **Where the guilds do their actual labor** — workshops, forges, warehouses, the markets

## The Texture

- **Worn streets** — the roads are eroded by weather and overloaded carts. Only [[Soma]] has invested in improvement (Paradise Avenue, the major artery running from the Port of Ondine up toward the Upper City).
- **Vertical slums** — parts of [[Soma]] lie inside **old Cynwäll quarries**, the most densely populated areas of the whole city. Dwellings stack on top of each other along streets too narrow for anything but foot traffic.
- **Surface sewer pipes** — because the ground is too unstable to bury them everywhere, the [[Guild of Architects|Architects]] ran great pipes along the surface. They crisscross the lower city. The poorest districts aren't connected at all and dump waste in the streets.
- **Choked traffic** — vendor carts and stalls clog the alleys. It's often faster to walk than to ride. Beggars and ruffians work the crowds.
- **Magical groves** — sprout anywhere, anytime, unpredictably. A good omen. Destroying one is a **crime**.

## Life Here

Most lower-city Cadwës face a hard choice: **guild service or day-to-day struggle**. Guild membership offers security at the cost of autonomy — you may never own your own shop, but you can be the indispensable assistant to a master and live comfortably. The alternative is the precarious freelance life: theft, smuggling, freelance labor, free-leaguing, or slow descent into the worst districts where refugees become "guinea pigs in the underground laboratories of unscrupulous alchemists" or — if they took an Usurer body-loan — "more or less consenting organ donors."

But it is **not necessarily worse than elsewhere**: the lower city is sheltered from the [[The Rag'narok|Rag'narok]] that devastates the rest of [[Aarklash]]. A refugee who survives the first hard years can build a real life. That tension — misery and shelter at once — is the engine of the [[Cadwë Dream]].

## Power in the Lower City

- The **eight lower fiefs** and their Peers
- The [[Guild of Thieves]] (HQ in [[Morgue Street]]), the [[Guild of Usurers]] (everywhere money is owed), the working guilds (Blacksmiths, Sewer Workers, Ferrymen)
- The [[Free Leagues]] — the shrews, heirs of the [[Dogs of War]], who actually defend the place
- The [[Militia]] — present, but half of it is on someone's payroll
- [[Sophet Drahas]]'s [[Court of Shadows]], reaching up from the catacrypts

## The Underground Boundary

The Lower City is the threshold to the [[Underground]]. Every catacrypt, sewer, and vestige-tunnel opens here. The [[The Sewers|sewers]], the [[Court of Ashes]], [[Glory-Hunt]], the [[Vegetal Entrails]], and the routes to the [[Labyrinth]] all begin in lower-city cellars, groves, and manholes. **The poor live closest to the things under the city.** They know more than they say.

> [!hook] The tide of the dead
> When heavy rain floods the underground, corpses from the common graves and old necropoli wash up into lower-city streets. The Cadwës lock their doors, boil their water, and wait. Necromancers use the flow. The [[Guild of Sewer Workers|Sewer Workers]] do the grim recovery. A free league working the lower city during a tide finds opportunity, horror, and the occasional body that *should not* be among the dead.
