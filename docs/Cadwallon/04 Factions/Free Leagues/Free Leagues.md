# Free Leagues

> "Mixing martial tradition, the spirit of free enterprise, and the taste for adventure, these individuals are called free leaguers."

The third pillar of Cadwallon's power — alongside the [[Houses of Peers]] and the [[Guilds]] — and the one the **player characters belong to**. The free leagues are companies of streetwise adventurer-mercenaries, the legal heirs of the [[Dogs of War]], who hold the formal power to **elect the Duke** and the formal duty to defend the city's independence.

## What They Are

Free leaguers are **licensed adventurers**. A league is a chartered company — registered, blazoned, and archived at the [[Great Hall]] at the [[Wall of Earth]]. The charter grants them rights ordinary Cadwës don't have (to bear arms, to investigate, to cross fief lines, to "step over the line of justice... as long as they pay for their crimes in gold") and binds them to duties (defense of the city, the free assembly, the renewal of allegiance to the Duke).

There are roughly **300–500 active free leaguers** across the city at any time — a small number against 200,000 Cadwës, but disproportionately important.

## History

- The original **[[Dogs of War]]** — 200 mercenaries in 21 companies under [[Vanius the Founder|Vanius]] — founded the city in 832
- Their heirs, too poor to settle, kept exploring the ruins; they were scornfully called **"the shrews"**
- In **983**, the great [[Akkyshan]] raid forced the city to rely on these forgotten heirs; they rose, organized the population, and pushed the elves back to the sea. This was **the rebirth of the leagues**.
- In **996**, Duke [[Duke Den Azhir|Den Azhir]] (himself a former leaguer) extended the **birthright and the free duty** — the modern legal foundation of the leagues
- For the past 20 years the leagues have reclaimed real power and prestige

## The Uncomfortable Truth

> [!secret] The leagues are not a stabilizing force
> The official story says the free leaguers guarantee Cadwallon's unity and independence. **The truth is the opposite.** The leagues are companies of street mercenaries who act alone, rarely show unity, and — through their feuds, their willingness to take dirty jobs, and their entanglement in Peer and guild intrigues — **build up the entropy that undermines the city.** A league deep in debt (fines eat the "prosperous duty") will take assassination, extortion, and burglary work. Some end up working, knowingly, for the [[Meanders of Darkness]]. The independence of the Free City is a pendulum, and the leagues are one of the weights that keeps it swinging dangerously.

## How a League Works

- **Charter & blazon** — registered at the [[Great Hall]]; the list of active leagues (and those waiting to be "re-decked") is displayed there
- **The prosperous duty** — leagues earn the right to operate; fines for criminal methods erode the treasury
- **The free assembly** — the annual gathering (1st of Odecime, before Freedom Week) where leaguers and the Duke meet
- **Allegiance renewal** — at the ducal jubilee, leaguers renew their oath to the Duke
- **"Consult Vanius"** — after probation, a league may pay (premium, 1d6 weeks' wait) for the services of a [[The Tarot-Mages|tarot-master]] at the Great Hall

## Relations

- **[[Duke Den Azhir]]** — patron and elected sovereign; himself a former leaguer (the [[Ruby-Heart]]). The Duke favors the leagues against the guilds.
- **[[Houses of Peers]]** — employers and entanglers; Peers hire leagues for deniable work
- **[[Guilds]]** — rivals and employers; the guilds resent the leagues' rights and blame them for "depredations"
- **[[Militia]]** — overlapping jurisdiction; professional friction; the militia regards leaguers as dangerously unaccountable
- **[[Guild of Blades]]** — the closest professional analog; many leaguers are ex-Blades and vice versa
