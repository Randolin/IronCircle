# Incarnates

> "After thousands of years in exile, the gods continue to wage war through their champions — Incarnates — and their most faithful allies."

The mortal champions of the gods. Since the gods themselves cannot walk [[Aarklash]] (the curse of Time forbids it — see [[The Game of the Gods]]), they act through **Incarnates**: mortals possessed, empowered, and made into living instruments of divine will. Incarnates are among the most dangerous beings in the [[The Rag'narok|Rag'narok]] — and Cadwallon has a peculiar relationship to them.

## What an Incarnate Is

An Incarnate is a mortal **chosen by a god as a channel** for its power and purpose on Aarklash. The god cannot manifest directly without suffering the curse of Time, so it pours a measure of itself into a champion who *can* act. The Incarnate gains tremendous power and becomes a focal point of the divine [[The Game of the Gods|game]].

Incarnation can be:
- **Sought** — a faithful champion offered up or aspiring to the role
- **Imposed** — a god seizing a useful mortal
- **Brief or lasting** — from a single decisive moment to a lifelong binding

## The Founding Incarnation: Vanius

The most important Incarnate in Cadwallon's history is [[Vanius the Founder|Vanius]]. When [[Sophet Drahas]]'s [[Acheron|Acheronian]] forces nearly took the elven ruins, [[Desire]] woke, panicked, and **chose Vanius as his Incarnation** — pouring his 22 divine elixirs into Vanius's tarot deck. Vanius wielded that power to win the [[Battle of the Wall of Earth]], then retained traces of the Incarnation even after Desire withdrew. *(see [[Vanius the Founder]], [[The 22 Arcana]])*

This is the model: a god, unable to act, reaches through a mortal at a decisive moment, and the mortal is never quite the same again.

## The Cadwallon Anomaly: The Unincarnable Duke

> [!secret] Why no god can take the Duke
> The gods have tried, repeatedly — "for fun, out of meanness, or as a challenge" — to **incarnate the Duke of Cadwallon. None ever managed.** The Duke is the **vessel of Cadwë spiritual energy**: the accumulated will of a city that refuses the [[The Rag'narok|Rag'narok]] and rejects the gods' game. That collective will fills the ducal seat so completely that no single god can force entry. The Duke is a **null square** on the divine board — which is exactly why the office is so strategically precious, and why [[Duke Den Azhir|Den Azhir]] being a [[dramatis]] (an already-shapeshifting, identity-fluid being) makes him an even stranger occupant of an already-strange throne. *(see [[Cosmology]], [[Duke Den Azhir]])*

## Incarnates in Cadwallon

True Incarnates rarely *reside* in Cadwallon — they are great travelers, celebrities of the [[The Rag'narok|Rag'narok]] who pass through the City of Thieves for a few days at a time. In game terms, **only exiled characters may count an Incarnate as a contact** — they are figures of continental significance, not neighborhood acquaintances.

Some Incarnates who touch Cadwallon's story:
- **Lyka'i the Freed** — the Wolfen bounty hunter of [[The Howlers]]; an Incarnate and Pariah
- Various **Contacts of the Rag'narok** — Incarnate celebrities representing the Confrontation/Rag'narok figures, who occasionally rest in the city
- [[Sophet Drahas]] — a being of comparable power, though his status is complicated by his curse

## Power Level

Incarnates sit at the very top of the power scale (Potential 10–13 in game terms — "the greatest heroes of the Rag'narok," who "can fend off the [[Militia]] of Cadwallon on their own"). A [[Free Leagues|free league]] does not fight an Incarnate and expect to win. It survives one, negotiates with one, or serves one.
