# The Game of the Gods

> "The gods of Aarklash are beyond the comprehension of any mortal and therefore they could not be explained."

The wider divine politics of [[Aarklash]] — the framework the Chroniclers use to understand the gods, and the cosmic stakes the [[The Rag'narok|Rag'narok]] is actually about. Cadwallon's local [[Conscience]]/[[Desire]] conflict is one move in a far older, larger game.

## The Chroniclers' Framework

The gods cannot be explained directly — over the ages each has been worshipped under dozens of names and aspects by successive civilizations. The **Chroniclers** (mysterious archivists of Aarklash's history) sidestep this by using **common nouns instead of names**: they speak of the gods as **primary forces** before they were "individuals." This expresses the idea that the gods *incarnate* fundamental concepts — the profound natures underlying the divine role in the [[Creation]].

This is why Cadwë cult-language uses abstractions (**Felicity, Pain, Destiny**) rather than proper deity names: those abstractions are closer to the truth than any single nation's pantheon.

## The Three Generations

The Chroniclers describe **three generations of gods**, which became (without anyone realizing) the names of the three Cadwë cults and dim echoes of the [[Aarklash#The Three Alliances|Three Alliances]]:

1. **Felicity** — the first, oldest powers
2. **Pain** — the second generation
3. **Destiny** — the third

The modern division of Aarklash into the **Ways of Light, Meanders of Darkness, and Paths of Destiny** is a worldly shadow of this divine genealogy.

## The Exile and the Curse of Time

The foundational event of all Aarklash cosmology:

- In the **age of the gods**, they traveled the [[Creation]] unrivaled, their empires spanning whole worlds
- **Discord came.** The gods warred for supremacy. The crumbling of dying worlds nearly collapsed the Creation entirely.
- **The Creation fought back** — it threw the gods from their thrones and exiled them beyond the borders of the elemental [[Realms]]
- **Time appeared** as the curse of their exile: if any god dares reappear openly on Aarklash, it suffers punishment and is *forgotten forever*

So the gods cannot act directly. They wage war through **proxies**: their champions (the [[Incarnates]]), their most faithful allies, and the slow erosion of the very curse that binds them.

## How the Gods Fight Now

> The balance is once again under threat. The curse of Time is **fading.** Darkness seizes the heart of the brave, and the gods get ready to lead their armies.

The gods can't walk Aarklash — so they fight through:

- **[[Incarnates]]** — mortals possessed and empowered to act as divine champions
- **Faithful allies** — the nations, cults, and Alliances that serve a god's interest
- **Dragons** — the great wyrms like [[Maloth]] and [[Vortiris]], born of [[Desire]], who took sides
- **The weakening of Time itself** — as the curse fades, the gods inch closer to direct intervention. **This is what the Rag'narok ultimately is**: the prophesied war that comes as the lock of Time loosens.

## Cadwallon's Place in the Game

Cadwallon is not a sideshow — it is **a board the whole game turns on**:

- It sits on [[Desire]]'s prison, making it the single most strategically vital location in the Creation
- Its [[The 22 Arcana|22 arcana]] are pieces of a god's essence, scattered across the board
- Its [[Duke Den Azhir|Duke]] is **unincarnable** — a vessel of Cadwë spiritual energy no god can possess, making the ducal seat a unique null-square in the divine game
- Its neutrality keeps the [[Conscience]]/[[Desire]] conflict frozen — and the frozen conflict keeps the world's lock intact

> [!secret] Why everyone is really here
> The emissaries, spies, and guild-alliances filling Cadwallon are not only chasing trade and treasure. **The gods' chosen few are pulling strings from the shadows, knowing the Free City keeps secrets that could collapse their enemies' beliefs.** Whoever controls Cadwallon's secrets — the arcana, the Labyrinth, Desire's prison — gains leverage in the Game of the Gods itself. *(see [[The Rag'narok]])*
