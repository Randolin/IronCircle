> "On the up, me. Straight as they come. Now do us a favour and stand aside, before I 'ave to be unfriendly about it."

**Concept:** A juiced-up orc/simian pit-fighter in a bowler hat, working the door and working his parole — a feral scrapper trying hard to go straight and not quite managing it. The trouble isn't only the addiction. Part of him *wants* to let go. Tooth, claw, and burning blood-rings; the worse the beating, the deadlier he gets. More anti-hero than hero — but he'll always rally for the crew.

## Look
Broad as a doorway and built like a siege engine — [[Orcs|orc]] heft with the long reach and grip of [[Simiah|simian]] blood, though small for a vat-batch (he was meant to be bigger). Tusked, scarred, eyes gone a little feral when the juice is up. Around each forearm, **searing rings of blood-light** smoulder and brighten as he takes punishment. Over all of it: a battered **bowler hat** and the careful, too-tidy manner of a man performing respectability. Cockney in the mouth, [[Syharhalna|New Shamir]] mutagen in the veins. They call him **"Breaker."** It used to be a promise. Now it's the thing he's trying not to do — to his parole, to furniture, to faces.

## Background

A **discarded [[Syharhalna|New Shamir]] vat-batch** — a runt, culled from the tanks and dumped, who had the bad manners to survive. He grew up gutter-feral on Cadwallon's streets until the **[[Guild of Thieves]]** scraped him up and poured him into the blood-sport circuit: the bottom-tier [[The Little Arenas|Little Arenas]] of [[Gamehead]] first, then the **Chains-and-Stone amphitheater** in [[Ogrokh]]. The Thieves kept their fighters winning with **mutagen decoctions** — highly addictive drugs that made him stronger, faster, and a great deal more feral. **The Guild owns his old fight-contract and his supply both.**

He got out of the pits, but he did not get *clean*. He ran with a crew, did real crime, and finally got pinched by the [[Militia]]. Now he's working off his sentence the Cadwë way — as a **free leaguer on "free duty"** (Den Azhir's 996 reform), a Militia handler holding the leash. **He cannot break parole again.** Between odd jobs he bounces the door at one of [[Jareth]]'s establishments, and lays low at **Lady Allya's Tavern** in [[The Rampart|the Muck]] — the one heated room in the lower city that'll seat a juiced orc, and the best place to hear things and pick up the kind of *lesser-evil* work a man on parole can almost justify.

## Drives

**Wants:** to stay out, stay clean-ish, keep the leash slack — to actually *be* the respectable doorman he's pretending to be.
**Bond:** the crew. He'll go feral for them without thinking twice — which is exactly the problem.
**Flaw — the beast is his, not the bottle's.** Brakka tells himself the rage is the mutagen: that if he could only get clean, he'd be a decent man. He's lying. Part of him *likes* the devolution — the blessed silence when the thinking stops and there's only the work of teeth and fists. The juice didn't make him this way, it just gave him a convenient excuse. Going straight isn't fighting an addiction. It's fighting **himself**, and he's losing slowly.

## Hooks
> [!secret] Made, then thrown away
> Brakka is a [[Syharhalna|New Shamir]] vat-batch — a *failed* one, discarded as a runt. That he lived, and then thrived enough to fight his way out of the pits, is exactly the sort of result the Scorpion technomancers would want back on a table for study. **New Shamir has a claim on its escaped mistake**, and the Meanders of Darkness are on the far end of that thread.

> [!hook] The Guild owns the leash
> The [[Guild of Thieves]] holds his old fight-contract *and* controls his juice. That's a stronger leash than parole — and the day they call it in, "free leaguer Brakka" and "Guild asset Breaker" are forced into the same room.

> [!hook] One more strike — and they know how
> His Militia handler is no friend. The next time the rage gets loose in public, free duty becomes a cell. A rival, a creditor, or an old pit-master who knows precisely how to **provoke the devolution** can end him without lifting a blade — just by getting him to be what he's trying not to be.
