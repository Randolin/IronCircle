# Ray Sato

> *The Watcher.*

**Pitch:** Ex-cop whose career Vera's paper ended. Became her believer and stalked her with a camera. A wall of photos of the victim, a gun, no alibi.
**Wants:** To finish her investigation himself.
**Gives:** dated photos, including one with a two-tone suit at the edge. Danger: decides who did it and acts on it.

## Detail

**Concept:** Ex-detective. Vera's zine broke the planted-evidence scandal that ended his career three years ago — and then, in the wreckage, he read her back issues and became her last convert. He's been shadowing her for months, running a parallel obsessive investigation of the disappearances out of a furnished room.

**Why he draws fire:** Motive (she ruined him), means (he kept a gun), no alibi, and — the gut-punch discovery — **surveillance photographs of Vera**, dated, including her last week. A bitter armed ex-cop with a wall of photos of the victim is the single most suspect-shaped object in noir.

**Actual guilt:** Stalking, obstruction, the original planted evidence. Not murder — he was her believer, not her enemy.

**The sting in the deflation:** one of his photo rolls, developed, holds a real clue the players can't get anywhere else — a long-lens frame of Vera at the Half Note, Day −10, with a lean man in a two-tone suit visible at the edge of the stand. Sato doesn't know what he has.

**After deflation:** A trained investigator with months of legwork, nothing left to lose, and a debt to the dead woman — hostile first, then the party's most dangerous friend. Or, played wrong, a man with a gun who reaches his own conclusion about who killed her.

**Visual:** Japanese-American man, late thirties — Nisei, one of the handful the postwar force ever hired and the first it threw away. Stern, contained, rectangular dark-framed glasses, cheap suit worn carefully, no badge but the posture never left.
