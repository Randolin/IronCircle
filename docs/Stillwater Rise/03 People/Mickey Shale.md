# Mickey Shale

> *The Judas.*

**Pitch:** Washed-up columnist who gave Vera her mocking nickname, then secretly helped her. He let slip about her list in a bar two weeks before she died.
**Wants:** To never open the letter she left him.
**Gives:** the tip chain, in his own voice, if someone's with him when he opens it. Clock: Day 10, alone or not.

## Detail

**Concept:** The youngest columnist the City ever had, washed up by forty-six. He didn't coin the nickname — old Emmett Roan did, and died — but Mickey inherited the column at twenty-eight and made his name *reviving* the bit, sharper and crueler, for a new audience. Betrayer, source, and unexploded confession.

**Motivation (specific):** Immediate: keep the letter in his coat unopened, because opening it makes it true. Beneath: one honest byline before the talent finishes rotting — the Vera story, told right, under his own name, in somebody's paper Weaver doesn't own. He knows there's no such paper. That's the joke he drinks at.

**Performable mannerisms:** Quotes his own old columns bitterly, verbatim. Buys rounds he can't afford on the Weaver expense account that still, mysteriously, works. Pats the breast pocket where the letter lives. Wears sunglasses indoors at the bar and knows exactly how that looks. Funny in the way of men performing at their own wake.

**Voice:**
- [deflecting]: "Roan coined it. I just — remodeled. Youngest column in the City and the first smart thing I did with it was a little girl's nickname. That's the business, kid. Buy me another."
- [the meetings]: "She let me help. You understand what that — she *let* me. Fifteen years and she just... put me to work like the debt was payable."
- [the letter, drunk]: "It's addressed in her hand. Whatever's in it, I earned it. That's exactly why it stays shut."

**Visual description:** White man, mid-forties, handsome gone soft rather than ruined — the byline photo is only twelve years old and still argues the point. Dark hair, early gray at the temples only. Dresses like the hotshot he was: sharp clothes a decade out of date, no hat, collar open, dark sunglasses parked on his face or his head. The letter's corner visible in the breast pocket, always.
