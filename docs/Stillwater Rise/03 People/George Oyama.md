# George Oyama

> *Records.*

**Pitch:** Records. Turns whatever the field brings back into evidence. Thoth: a false document feels physically wrong to him.
**Wants:** To catch a machine that disappears people through paperwork.
**Gives:** the order the lies were told in. Doesn't know what he is. The chair across from him is empty.

## Detail

- **Look:** Japanese-American, early forties, calm full face, a premature gray streak in neat black hair. Cardigan, small bow tie, one ink-darkened finger.
- **Manner:** Reads documents upside down across desks. Handles paper by the edges. Dates everything, including his lunch bag. Gets quieter, not louder, when a document lies.
- **Heart Mountain,** 1942–45, as a kid. 'I've seen a government file a family into a horse stall. Took four forms. Whoever built this used five.'
- **Mythos:** Thoth. The record is the thing that happened; a false one feels wrong. He doesn't know. He feels the cold when the referral arrives.
- **The other chair:** empty since he came. 'The last records man's.' Nobody remembers the name. Beyond this arc.

**Voice:**
- [on the docket]: "Routing stamps are a handwriting. This one's ambidextrous. That's not a compliment."
- [method]: "Paper doesn't lie. People lie, and then they make paper, and the paper remembers what order they lied in."
- [the once, quietly]: "I've seen a government file a family into a horse stall. Took four forms. Whoever built this used five. Improvement, I suppose."
