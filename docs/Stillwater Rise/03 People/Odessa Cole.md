# Odessa Cole

> *Operations.*

**Pitch:** Runs the agency. Former Army nurse. Assignments, deadlines, cover. No Mythos, on purpose; she's the normal one who keeps the office looking normal.
**Wants:** To not bury a third case that deserved daylight.
**Gives:** one more day, one more favor. Suspects she isn't really in charge and has chosen not to check.

## Detail

**Concept:** The agency's spine. Former Army nurse — one of the units nobody made movies about — who ran forward surgical tents and now runs casework the same way: triage, worst bleeding first, feelings later. The one who pulled the strings that got the team the pier.

**Motivation (specific):** The agency's mandate survives on results and discretion, in that order, and she has buried two cases in her career that deserved daylight because the agency wouldn't have survived the exposure. Vera's case reads to her like a third — and she has privately decided she is not burying another one. The players will never hear this speech; they'll only notice she keeps finding them one more day, one more favor, one more piece of cover.

**Performable mannerisms:** Answers the phone mid-first-ring. Writes in a shorthand only she reads. States the time cost of everything ("That's a two-day favor you're asking for"). Has already thought of the thing you're about to say, and waits politely for you to finish saying it anyway.

**Voice:**
- [briefing]: "Homicide didn't want it, which is the first interesting thing about it. Second interesting thing's in the coroner's van. Go."
- [cover]: "You have until Thursday before somebody upstairs asks me why. Make me a liar by Wednesday."
- [the tell]: "I've closed cases that should've stayed open. Don't ask which. Just understand why this one's different, and don't waste it."

**Visual description:** Black woman, late forties, square-shouldered, close-pressed hair pinned back, a silk scarf knotted at the throat of a tailored jacket — the one soft thing on an otherwise operational silhouette. A small mole at the corner of her mouth; reading everything at a glance and filing it.

**Logos, all the way down — and the most capable one in the building.** Cole has no Mythos. [[The Judge]] keeps her for exactly that reason: a judgment needs a mortal witness, and a court run entirely by the uncanny stops being believed. She is the illusion's load-bearing wall. Nine years ago the first inquest referral arrived in her in-tray with the Coroner's stamp and six lines on it. She read it, solved it, and decided not to wonder. She has not wondered since, out loud. Privately she keeps a count of the dockets in the back of her shorthand book, and the count is the only thing in the office she has never told Blum.

**What she suspects:** that the agency is not hers, that the three new hires were not her idea in the way she remembers them being her idea, and that the retainer is a story told to her so she'll have something to protect. She has decided she doesn't care as long as the cases are real. If a player ever asks her who the Coroner is, she answers honestly: "Never met him. Nine years." Then, after a beat: "I've met the clerk. Don't make me think about that."

**In the arc:** the two buried cases were dockets she couldn't close. The Judge did not punish her. She has been waiting nine years to find out whether that was mercy or filing.
