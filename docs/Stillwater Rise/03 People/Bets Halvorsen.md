# Bets Halvorsen

> *The wagon.*

**Concept:** Drives the coroner's van. Twenty years of picking up the City's dead from piers, stairwells, and kitchens. Talks to them in the back while she drives, and says they answer. Everyone treats it as her joke. It isn't.

**Motivation (specific):** Get every one of them where they're going. She thinks of the run to the morgue as the last ride anybody gets and she takes it seriously: no speeding, radio off, and she tells them where they are at each turn. Immediate: nine runs in four years "felt wrong," like the passenger wasn't in the back, and she wants somebody to finally ask her about it.

**Performable mannerisms:** Talks over her shoulder to the empty van. Chews licorice. Names every corner as she takes it. Won't let anyone ride in the back with a body ("It's their seat"). Keeps a notebook of runs in the glove box: date, address, weather, and one word about the passenger.

**Voice:**
- [the pier, Day 1]: "She's quiet. They're usually loud the first hour. This one's just... already gone somewhere. You want to know how many times I've said that? Nine. I write it down."
- [the joke that isn't]: "I don't hear voices. I hear passengers. There's a difference and I'm not going to explain it to a man holding a clipboard."
- [alliance]: "You want the nine? Glove box. Dates and weather. Don't lose it, that's twenty years of people."

**Mythos:** Charon's daughter, more or less. The dead don't lie to her, and they don't hide from her either. When a body in her van is *empty*, she knows it, and she has never had a word for what that means until now. Her notebook is the only independent list of the folded-clothes deaths that exists outside the precinct, and it includes two the precinct never filed.

**Visual description:** Broad, weather-beaten white woman in her fifties, Norwegian stock, iron-gray hair in a single braid under a coroner's cap, a man's work jacket over the county uniform, licorice in the breast pocket. Hands like a dockhand's. Kind, tired, entirely unembarrassed.

**Use:** The players' first ally, because she is already on the pier at 6 a.m. on Day 1 and nobody has ever asked her anything. Her notebook is a route into the pattern that bypasses [[Frank Casale|Casale]], [[Ruth Halloran|Halloran]], and [[Nathaniel Pell|Pell]] entirely. Cost: none the first time. After that, she wants to be told where the passengers went. She'll know if the answer's a lie.

**Danger:** If Weaver learns the notebook exists, it goes on the paper column. Bets is buyable in theory and not in practice, which moves her toward the water column faster than anyone expects.
