# The Judge

> *The one who reads the docket.*

**Concept:** The City's watcher. Enma-ō, king of the court of the dead, whose mirror shows a soul as it is. Every death in the City is a case in his court and every case must be *heard*: named, weighed, filed. He has held this jurisdiction longer than the City has had a name, and he will hold it after Stillwater Rise is a plaza. The agency is one of his instruments. It is not the only one, and nobody at the agency knows that.

**The Logos, such as it is:** The Hon. Ambrose Yanagi, retired justice of the county court of records, listed as deceased in 1948. His signature still appears on documents that need one. Nobody living remembers meeting him. If a player digs, they find a photograph of a courtroom with an empty bench and a clerk looking at it.

**Motivation (specific, and larger than this arc):** The City's ledger does not balance. Every year a number of its dead are never named, never weighed, never filed: they go into the river, the foundation, the incinerator, the relocation program, and the court never hears them. That is not merely injustice to him. It is a *debt* on the City, and something is spending the difference. He does not yet know what. Weaver's bargain is one symptom; there have been others, in other decades, and there will be more. He selects agents who can see through the Mist because the unfiled dead are hidden behind it, and he keeps mortals on staff because a judgment requires a mortal witness and because a court that looks invulnerable stops being believed.

**Why these three:** The mirror shows him anyone who has stood at the river and been sent back. [[Arthur Rook]], [[Zu Cheng]], and [[Jack Rivers]] each nearly died once. He saw what they were at the crossing. He put their names on a list, and the list arrived in [[Odessa Cole|Cole]]'s in-tray with no postmark.

**How he acts:** On paper only. A docket sheet appears: a name, a date, six lines. A verdict slip arrives after a name has been read aloud on the record: the name, and one word, *heard*. He never telephones, never writes a sentence, never explains, never intervenes. If the players are drowning, he does not save them; he files them. Cole has worked for him nine years and believes he is a committee.

**The illusion:** [[The Auberon Trust]] is real, mundane, and vulnerable on purpose. Weaver can reach a trustee; Blum can hold it a week; the mandate can be pulled. He permits all of it. A court that cannot be threatened would be noticed.

**Performable presence (he is never in the room):** The in-tray. A signature nobody remembers. Cold in the office the morning a docket arrives; [[Pearl Nazario|Pearl]] complains about the radiator. Oyama gets quiet around the paper and doesn't know why.

**Voice:** None spoken. Written, ever:
- [the docket]: *Kestrel, Vera. F. 38. Flats. Unheard.*
- [the verdict slip]: *Heard.*
- [once, on the back of a docket, in the same hand, to nobody]: *Eleven outstanding.*

**Visual description:** Do not describe him. If it becomes unavoidable: a very tall figure at the far end of a corridor with the light behind him, a judge's robe or a good black coat, a face the eye slides off. In the photograph, an empty bench.

**Beyond the arc:** He is the campaign's spine across arcs. Each arc is a case his court could not hear. Whatever is spending the difference in the City's ledger is the long game, and he is not certain it is a single thing. The empty chair across from [[George Oyama|Oyama]]'s desk belonged to someone; see Oyama.
