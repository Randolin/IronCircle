# The Judge

> *The Coroner. Nobody remembers voting for him.*

**Concept:** The County Coroner, Ambrose Yanagi. He has held the office since before anyone at the agency was born, is re-elected unopposed every four years, and has never been photographed. His orders come out of the [[County Coroner|Coroner's office]] on paper, over a signature. Underneath: Enma-ō, judge of the dead. Every death in the City is supposed to come before him. Lately, a lot of them don't.

**What he's actually doing:** He is losing. A soul that dies unnamed and unheard never reaches his court, and he has worked out that they aren't just lost. **Something else is taking them.** The Piper walks people into the river and they never come out; that's twelve that went to someone who isn't him. Weaver buys stories and the tellers vanish; more. For a century the City's unaccounted dead have been going *somewhere*, and whatever is collecting them has grown on it. He cannot leave his court to fight for them. The agency is how he fights. Every name read aloud on the record is a soul pulled back to his side of the river.

**Why these three:** His mirror shows him anyone who has stood at the river and been sent back. [[Arthur Rook]], [[Zu Cheng]], and [[Jack Rivers]] each nearly died once. He saw what they were at the crossing and put their names on a list. The list arrived at [[Odessa Cole|Cole]]'s desk as a memo from the Coroner's office recommending three hires.

**How he acts:** On paper only. An inquest referral arrives from the Coroner's office: a name, a date, six lines, and a stamp. When a name has been read aloud on the record, a one-line closing notice follows: the name, and the word *heard*. He never telephones, never explains, never appears. If the players are drowning, he does not save them. Cole has worked for him nine years and has never been in the same room as him.

**The cover:** the Coroner's office is a county budget line, and the [[Renewal Authority]] has friends on the county board. The retainer can be cut with one vote. He lets it be that vulnerable on purpose. A coroner who could not be leaned on would get noticed.

**Presence (he is never in the room):** the referral in the in-tray. The stamp. Cold in the office the morning it arrives; [[Pearl Nazario|Pearl]] blames the radiator. [[George Oyama|Oyama]] goes quiet around the paper and doesn't know why. [[Ruth Halloran|Halloran]] technically reports to him and has met only his chief clerk.

**Voice:** None spoken. Written:
- [the referral]: *Kestrel, Vera. F. 38. Flats. Inquest referred. Unheard.*
- [the closing notice]: *Heard.*
- [once, on the back of a referral, in the same hand]: *Eleven outstanding. They are not lost.*

**Visual description:** Do not describe him. If it becomes unavoidable: a very tall figure at the far end of the Coroner's corridor with the window behind him, a good black coat, a face the eye slides off.

**Beyond the arc:** He is the campaign's spine. Each arc is a case where the dead went somewhere they shouldn't have. The thing collecting them is the long villain; it has no name yet, and Weaver and Bunting work for it without knowing, the way the agency works for him without knowing. The empty chair across from Oyama's desk belonged to someone; see Oyama.
