# Adaeze Okonkwo

> *The record.*

**Pitch:** Inquest stenographer. What she types is the record. Has been told to strike testimony four times and remembers all four word for word.
**Wants:** The record to be true.
**Gives:** the struck passages, to Blum only. The finale runs through her hands. Danger: the inquest gets rescheduled away from her.

## Detail

**Concept:** Inquest stenographer for the [[County Coroner]]. Nigerian, fifties, church hat, never once rattled in eleven years of hearing the worst thing that happened to somebody read into the record. What she types *is* the record. That makes her the most important person in the building for a judge who needs a name said aloud and written down.

**Motivation (specific):** The record has to be true. She has been told to strike testimony from the transcript four times in eleven years, and she struck it, because that's the job. She also remembers all four, word for word, and has never been asked. Immediate: the Kestrel inquest is on her calendar, and she has noticed that the folded-clothes inquests all ran short.

**Performable mannerisms:** Hands never leave the machine while anyone is speaking. Corrects nobody, ever, out loud. Looks at the ceiling when she's remembering something struck. Brings a thermos of tea and does not share it. Church hat on the desk beside her, always, like a second witness.

**Voice:**
- [the job]: "I type what is said. If the Coroner strikes it, I strike it. What I remember is my own business."
- [asked about the short inquests]: "Twelve minutes. Nine minutes. Eleven. A jumper inquest takes forty if anyone asks a question. Nobody asked a question. I noticed. I'm paid to type, not to notice, so I typed."
- [the finale, if it comes to her]: "Say the name again. Slower. I want it spelled right on the first page and the last."

**Visual description:** Tall, composed Nigerian woman in her fifties, dark skin, hair covered by a wide-brimmed church hat in navy, a matching suit with a brooch, reading glasses on a chain. A stenotype in a leather case. Posture like a pillar.

**Use:** The finale runs through her hands. Whatever platform the players choose, the inquest is the one where a name read aloud becomes a county record the same afternoon, and she is the one who types it. Earlier: the four struck passages are in her memory and nowhere else; two of them are from folded-clothes inquests. Cost: she'll recite them for [[Esther Blum|Blum]], because Blum is counsel of record and that's proper. Not for the players directly. Not ever.

**Danger:** Weaver can't buy her and can't drown her without an inquest noticing, so Weaver tries to have the Kestrel inquest *rescheduled* into a room where Okonkwo isn't the stenographer. [[Maeve Brogan|Brogan]] can do that with a phone call. Watch for it.
