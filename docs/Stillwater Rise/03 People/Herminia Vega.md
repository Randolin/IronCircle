# Herminia Vega

> *The next name.*

**Pitch:** Widow on the fourth floor of a Delancey walk-up who refused the buyout three times. Vera circled her name with a date. Pearl's aunt.
**Wants:** To stay in her apartment.
**Clock:** the knock Day 8, the pier Day 9. Saving her moves the date; it doesn't erase it.

## Detail

**Concept:** Not a myth, not a witness, not a source. A sixty-one-year-old widow on the fourth floor of a Delancey block walk-up who has refused the buyout three times, and whose name is circled in Vera's last notebook with a date written beside it: *the 9th.* She exists so the pattern stops being historical.

**Motivation (specific):** Stay in the apartment. Her husband died in the back bedroom, her sister lives two doors down the hall, and the relocation office has offered her an address in a city she has never heard of. She does not think of this as resistance and would be embarrassed to hear it called courage. She thinks of it as her apartment.

**Performable mannerisms:** Feeds people within ninety seconds of their arrival, non-negotiably. Asks questions and then answers them herself. Keeps the three buyout letters in a drawer — read the first, glanced at the second, filed the third unopened. Knows every tenant in the building by floor and by grievance. Turns her hearing aid down when men in suits start talking, and does not hide doing it.

**Voice:**
- [at the door]: "You're not from the office. The office knocks like it owns the hallway. Come in, there's coffee, take your shoes off, my sister mopped."
- [the letters]: "The first one I read. The second one I looked at. The third one I put with the second one. Is there a new word in the third one? Did they think of a new word?"
- [Day 8, the alarm]: "A man came this morning. Very polite, very quiet. He said I should pack the good dishes first, because movers are careless with the good dishes. How does he know I have good dishes?"

**Visual description:** Small, broad Puerto Rican woman of sixty-one, gray hair in a short home set, housedress under a buttoned cardigan regardless of season, thick-soled house slippers she wears into the hallway. Reading glasses on a beaded chain. A thin gold wedding band worn thin.
