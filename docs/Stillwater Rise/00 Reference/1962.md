# 1962

Period cheat sheet. From the recovered [[The Rat Line (draft-2)|draft-2]] doc, lightly trimmed.

## Why this year

Urban renewal at peak arrogance and no organized opposition yet, so Vera is alone. One person can still own paper, radio, and TV. The jazz-club economy is visibly fading, so Bunting's decline is current. Switchboards still run on people, so Mabel and Rimm exist. Mimeograph counter-media predates the underground press, so Vera is a crank, not a movement. Forensics are weak enough to protect the mystery.

## Texture, always on

- Rotary phones, call boxes, party lines. Hats on men, cigarettes everywhere, cash for everything.
- Records are paper, held in buildings, fetched in person from clerks with moods.
- Cars are boats; the El rattles; neon buzzes. Black-and-white TV; radio still respectable.
- Flashbulb cameras. Film needs developing, hours at a lab, and someone at the lab sees your negatives.

## Things that don't exist

- No security cameras or footage, anywhere.
- No caller ID, no answering machines. Messages go through services or desk clerks.
- No databases. "Run this name" is a person digging through folders.
- No DNA, no trace-fiber magic, no precise time of death. Tox screens catch alcohol, barbiturates, opiates. Crude and slow.
- No Miranda (1966). Cops question hard, hold people on nothing, and nobody's shocked.

## The six rulings players will force

1. **"Trace the call."** Only through a human operator, only if it's still connected or logged. The Exchange is the choke point. Mabel appreciates in value every time they ask.
2. **"Pull the records."** Walk to the building. Charm or badge the clerk. Wait. Hours to days; the wait is playable time, and which clerk saw them asking is information someone sells.
3. **"Photograph the evidence."** Film camera, flashbulbs indoors, lab development. The eyes fog every print, and the lab tech talks about the weird ones.
4. **"Ballistics?"** Yes. Rifling comparison under a microscope is period-real and reliable. That's why the piling slug matters.
5. **"Fingerprints?"** Real but manual. Matching needs a suspect card to compare against. A print is worthless until they have a name.
6. **"Canvass for witnesses."** Gold. This era's forensic engine is people who saw things and the legwork to find them. The game rewards shoe leather by design.

## The racial dimension

Real urban renewal in 1962 was deeply racialized; "Negro removal" was the bitter joke of the day. The Flats is Black and Puerto Rican at the core, with Jewish shopkeepers and aging German and Irish holdouts, and the Authority's org chart is all white.
