# The Auberon Trust

> Whoever sits above Cole. Whoever sits above that.

A charitable trust endowed by an old shipping family, chartered "for inquiries in the public interest." It has retained [[The Agency]] since 1953 through a lattice [[Esther Blum|Blum]] built and nobody else has read. It is the reason the agency can take cases nobody pays for, and the reason it can be stopped by a telephone call.

It is also the illusion. The Trust is real, mundane, and deliberately vulnerable. Above it, unlisted, is [[The Judge]]. The Trust exists so that the agency has something ordinary people can threaten.

## How it works

- Three trustees. Two are elderly and sign what the third puts in front of them. The third is a corporate lawyer named **Whitcombe Ard**, who plays squash with the [[Renewal Authority]]'s counsel and has appeared twice on [[Vivian Weaver|Weaver]]'s afternoon program.
- The founding instrument was drafted in 1953 and bears a witness signature: *A. Yanagi, J.* Ard has never noticed. Blum has, and stopped asking.
- [[Odessa Cole|Cole]] reports quarterly to the trustees. The dockets do not come from the trustees. They arrive in her in-tray with no postmark, and she has decided, nine years ago, not to wonder.
- The retainer can be withdrawn on thirty days' notice or suspended immediately "pending review of a conflict."

## In the arc

This is the paper column's route to the agency's mandate ([[Danger Board]]). Weaver's Day 7 call goes to Ard. Her lever is a conflict of interest: an agency employee ([[Pearl Nazario]]) with family on a docket the agency is investigating. Blum can hold it for a week. After that the case continues unsanctioned, or it stops.

The Judge lets it happen. An unsanctioned agency still files.
