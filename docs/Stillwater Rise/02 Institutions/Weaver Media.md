# Weaver Media

> She owns everything that's ever been said about you.

The morning paper, the radio hour, the afternoon program, Channel 6, assembled from silk money in under six years. [[Vivian Weaver]] at the top; [[Walt Herzog]] on the city desk; [[Preston Day]] on the air (and being eclipsed); [[Mickey Shale]] what's left of a columnist.

## The web

The City's wires are a web and Weaver sits at the center. Telephone (the [[The Exchange|Exchange]] carries it whether it wants to or not), radio, television, newsprint. The relocation program feeds it: each person the Piper walks into the river leaves paper and noise behind, the notice, the docket, the harbor log, four inches on page seven, a joke on the afternoon program, and the soul rides that publicity into the wires. Weaver takes what it knew as it passes. Then it goes on, down a line out of the City that she has never followed. See [[The Case#The Web]].

- **What travels the web:** anything said on a telephone, broadcast, or printed. Weaver knows it eventually.
- **What doesn't:** face to face, handwritten and never mailed, spoken memory ([[Dez Okafor|Okafor]]), the same account told the same way ([[Cutty Sloan|Cutty]]), and [[August Klein|Klein]]'s archive, which the wire didn't make.
- **Who keeps it running:** [[Gerry Fitch|Fitch]], with the paper and the publicity cycle. Herzog and Day don't know what they're carrying.
- **Who can cut it:** [[Hal Rimm|Rimm]], once he knows what his tapes are.
