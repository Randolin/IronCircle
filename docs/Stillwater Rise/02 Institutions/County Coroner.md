# County Coroner

> Inquest referred.

The county office that rules on every death in the City that isn't obviously natural. An inquest is not a police case, which is why Homicide can hand one off with a shrug and why an outside inquiry firm can stand at a body with a signed pass.

## How it works

- The **Coroner**, Ambrose Yanagi, is elected. He runs unopposed, has never been photographed, and issues everything through his chief clerk on paper. See [[The Judge]].
- **[[Ansel Morrow]]**, chief clerk. The only person who has been in a room with the Coroner. Delivers referrals by hand.
- **Dr. [[Ruth Halloran]]**, Medical Examiner. Reports to the Coroner; has met only the clerk. Signs the scene pass.
- **[[Bets Halvorsen]]**, wagon driver. Twenty years of pickups and a notebook of every run. Talks to the passengers.
- **[[Teddy Ferrante]]**, scene photographer, freelance. Shoots everything; keeps the negatives.
- **[[Adaeze Okonkwo]]**, inquest stenographer. What she types is the record.
- **[[The Agency]]** is a licensed, bonded inquiry firm on retainer to this office for inquest inquiries. Referrals arrive as a six-line carbon with the office stamp. Halloran signs the agency's scene pass; the precinct honors it because it has to.
- The office is a **county budget line**. The [[Renewal Authority]] has friends on the county board's finance committee, chaired by [[Whitcombe Ard]], who has appeared twice on [[Vivian Weaver|Weaver]]'s afternoon program.

## In the arc

This is the paper column's route to the agency ([[Danger Board]]). Weaver's Day 7 call goes to Ard: the agency has an employee ([[Pearl Nazario]]) with family on a docket it's investigating, a "conflict." Ard moves to suspend the retainer pending review. [[Esther Blum|Blum]] can hold it a week on procedure. After that the agency keeps working without a pass, or it stops.

The Coroner lets it be that fragile. A coroner nobody could lean on would get noticed.
