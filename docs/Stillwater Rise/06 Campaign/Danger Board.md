# Danger Board

Who is at risk **next.** Profiles live in [[People]]; the next name is [[Herminia Vega]]. Windows are Day N per the [[Timeline]]. Nobody on this list has been taken yet — that is the entire point of the list. Reread at the top of every session and move at least one entry forward.

It sorts by **who wants them gone**, not by method, because the three principals kill for completely different reasons and almost never want the same person removed. That makes the board a diagnostic: *which* name gets taken tells the players which of the three is moving, weeks before they can prove anything about any of them.

## The three kill logics

**Bunting kills for rent.** He has no enemies and no appetite. He takes the docket the Authority hands him and tells himself they all owed something. Vera was the first who didn't, and he panicked. He isn't hunting anyone; he's hiding. The danger from him is the docket running on schedule regardless, and the rats, which protect him whether he asks or not. Corner him and the room fills.

**Maas kills nobody.** He wants people *gone*, believes the relocation program is real, and declines to check. His threat is institutional: lawyers, hearings, delay, an inquest rescheduled, a career ended, a contract not renewed. If someone in his way is physically harmed, it genuinely wasn't him — and that fact is an instrument the players can use, because it is the one thing about himself he can be made to prove.

**Weaver harvests.** People are the crop and the web is the machine. A relocated soul rides the publicity of its own death into her wires and she takes what it knew. So she wants the docket to run, the paper to print, and the program to air, on schedule, every time. She kills to protect the web's integrity: anyone who can hear it, print against it, or speak what it can't carry. Records she can buy, she buys; records that are people, she has walked in, and takes what they knew on the way through.

The two interlock: Weaver decides, Fitch routes, Bunting performs. A name on her water column becomes a job on his docket. He never hears her name. Every collection makes her stronger.

## Maas's obstacles — nobody dies

He does not have a kill list. He has a legal department, and it is genuinely dangerous.

- **Dr. Ruth Halloran** — her signature on nine files is a career-ending liability the Authority can invoke whenever it needs her quiet. She knows it. That is the entire reason the memo has sat in her desk for two years.
- **Det. Frank Casale** — seven years from a pension, in a department the Authority pays stipends into. He does not need to be threatened; he needs to be *reminded*, once.
- **The agency** — Blum's lattice of retainers and 1911 precedents is load-bearing and, examined by a well-funded firm, not unbreakable.
- **Ida** — the gap. She is the one person who could put the invoice on his desk in public, and the one person he would never permit to be touched. Weaver fills that gap without telling him. Learning that it was filled is what breaks him in the endgame, and it is the only thing that will.

## The ricochet — nobody aimed these

- **Sal Lupo.** Finds Bunting before the players do. Either he loses his crew in an alley or he becomes a corpse that muddies every timeline the players have built. His hunt runs on its own schedule; slowing it down is a task.
- **Ray Sato.** Reaches a conclusion and acts on it. Armed, trained, months into legwork, and wrong about one crucial thing. Whoever he decides did it does not get a trial.
- **Tin City.** Ottilie's camp is on no docket because it is on no list. That is exactly why the embankment can be cleared without a single signature, and why forty-one people can stop existing in an afternoon with nobody having ordered anything.

## Using it

**One rule keeps this from being a treadmill: saving someone costs something specific and visible.** Herminia's date moves when the players intervene — it does not vanish, and Bunting adds the interference to his accounting. Getting Cutty off the street means housing him somewhere, which means somebody knows where he is. Pulling Mabel out of the switchboard ends the flow of overheard calls. The board should never be beatable by general competence; it should be beatable one name at a time, at prices the table argues about.

**And read the column, not just the name.** If someone who belongs in the paper column turns up in the water instead, Weaver has stopped being careful — which is the loudest signal the arc can send without anyone saying anything out loud.
