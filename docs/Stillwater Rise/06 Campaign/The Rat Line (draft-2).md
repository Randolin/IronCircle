## The Rat Line
*A period-noir murder mystery arc — the City, 1962.*

**Theme:** the disappeared were people with no story, so nobody missed them. The victim died trying to give them one.

**The three tiers:**
1. **Piper** — who killed her, and how. Resolution: identity + method + mandate (he was *hired*).
2. **Maas** — the machine. Resolution: the disappearances are authorized policy, not a glitch — and this line item didn't originate inside the Authority.
3. **Weaver** — the author. Resolution: intent, motive, and the voice on the wire.

**The clock:** the demolition schedule IS the kill calendar — collections fall on the eve of each block's clearance, and the schedule is accelerating toward the ribbon-cutting. Toby's block is on it.

## The Year: 1962 — Period Cheat Sheet

**Why this year:** urban renewal at peak arrogance (no organized opposition yet — Vera is alone); one man can still own paper + radio + TV (Weaver's trifecta dies by decade's end); the jazz-club economy is visibly fading (Bunting's decline is current, not ancient); switchboards still run on people (Mabel and Rimm exist); mimeograph counter-media predates the underground press (Vera is a crank, not a movement); and forensics are weak enough to protect the mystery (no cameras, crude tox, pre-Miranda policing).

### Texture — always on
- Rotary phones, call boxes, party lines. Hats on men, cigarettes everywhere, cash for everything.
- Records are paper, held in buildings, fetched in person from clerks with moods.
- Cars are boats; the El rattles; neon buzzes. Black-and-white TV, radio still respectable.
- Flashbulb cameras; film needs developing — hours at a lab, *and someone at the lab sees your negatives.*

### Things that don't exist (don't let habit invent them)
- No security/CCTV footage of anything, anywhere.
- No caller ID, no answering machines (messages go through services or desk clerks).
- No computer databases — no "run this name" that isn't a person digging through folders.
- No DNA, no usable trace-fiber magic, no reliable time-of-death precision. Tox screens catch alcohol, barbiturates, opiates — crude, slow.
- No Miranda (1966): cops question hard, hold people on nothing, and nobody's shocked.

### The six rulings players will force
1. **"Trace the call."** Only through a human operator, only if it's still connected or logged — the Exchange is the choke point. Mabel appreciates in value every time they ask.
2. **"Pull the records."** Walk to the building. Charm or badge the clerk. Wait. Hours to days; the wait is playable time, and which clerk saw them asking is information someone sells.
3. **"Photograph the evidence."** Film camera, flashbulbs indoors, lab development. The eyes fog every print — and the lab tech talks about the weird ones.
4. **"Ballistics?"** Yes — rifling comparison under a microscope is period-real and reliable. This is why the piling slug matters.
5. **"Fingerprints?"** Real but manual: matching requires a suspect card to compare against, not a database sweep. A print is worthless until they have a *name*.
6. **"Canvass for witnesses."** Gold. This era's actual forensic engine is people who saw things and the legwork to find them. The game rewards shoe leather by design.

### The racial dimension (decide on purpose)
Real-world urban renewal in 1962 was deeply racialized — "Negro removal" was the bitter contemporary joke, and the Flats (Bunting's stolen royalties, Dez's griot tradition) already gestures at that history. Two valid table choices: engage it honestly for weight, or keep the district a period-flavored ethnic mosaic and play lighter. Choose consciously; don't drift.

## Cast

### Manny Bunting — the Piper (killer)
- **Mundane:** Tenor sax session man; played on records everyone knows, credited on none. Royalties stolen, union pension gone. Day work: "relocation specialist" contracted through the Renewal Authority — knocks on doors ahead of the wrecking ball and tenants leave. Two-tone suit, yellow and red. Soft-spoken, punctual, keeps immaculate books.
- **Mythos:** The Piper of Hamelin. Rift rule: *he will never be paid for the music, only the removal.* Collection is the one invoice that always clears.
- **Method:** A tune, planted once (a solo, a whistle in passing). The mark completes it over days, then walks calm into the river at night. Block collections fall on the **eve of demolition** — the night before the wrecking ball; contract jobs (like Vera) run on their own deadlines.
- **Moral position:** Genuinely owed by every institution that robbed him — and he collects from the poor on behalf of the rich, because the real debtors are uncollectable and tenants aren't. He aims his legend where it's easy. That's the line sympathy doesn't cross.
- **Current state:** Vera was his first failure ever — one unsettled ledger entry, one panicked shot in the shoulder before the kill shot. He's been playing flat on Thursdays since. Cracks are new to him.
- **Open thread:** where the successfully collected *go*. He may believe something about it ("resettled") that he's never verified either.

### Vera Kestrel — the victim
- **Mundane:** Counter-media activist; ran *The Plain Truth*, a mimeographed weekly. Records requests, hearing heckles, corkboard journalism. Citywide joke — nickname coined thirty years ago by Weaver's columnist after she, at eight years old, said the true thing on an open mic during the televised unveiling of the Renewal model. Never disbelieved; *laughed at.*
- **Mythos:** The child from *The Emperor's New Clothes* — sees through appearances; saying so makes her a joke.
- **Death:** The tune took her walking, but at the waterline her Rift cracked the glamour — the passage failed. Bunting, watching, shot her: shoulder (panic), back of head (kill), one miss. First body ever recovered in the pattern.
- **What she left:** the archive; the notation on August's roll; the half-cut stencil; the hidden card-drawer file; a mailed letter that surfaces in Act Two.

### Sterling Maas — Midas (the machine)
- **Mundane:** Renewal Commissioner. The City's Moses — expressways, plazas, the gilt Model Room. Sincere believer in the golden future.
- **Mythos:** Midas. Marginally literal touch (things left overnight in the Model Room). Donkey ears: cannot tell good counsel from bad — which is why Fitch owns his ear.
- **Moral position — HE KNOWS.** He knows what the Piper does and that the cleared don't resurface. He authorizes it as a necessary evil, insulated by a load-bearing euphemism ("relocation") he has chosen never to examine. He signs the docket knowing what signing does and ignoring who's on it. Vera was a line item; asked about her, his honest answer is *"who?"*
- **The flip:** Not guilt — *betrayal.* Shown that Fitch is Weaver's and that his machine was used for a private killing, he turns on Weaver out of pride and control, cooperating only in ways that protect the machine. Devil's-bargain ally.
- **Open thread:** the daughter, **Ida Maas** — a records clerk in his own Authority until two years ago, when she saw a clearance ledger she wasn't meant to and quit without explaining. He calls it ingratitude. Now a settlement-house caseworker. She has documents, or knows where they live.

### Vivian "Viv" Weaver (née Zhu) — Anansi (the author)
- **Mundane:** Morning paper, radio, and the City's first TV channel, one roof — plus her own afternoon television program: the City's young tastemaker, silk-fortune heiress turned media darling. "Weaver" is her chosen broadcast name. Collects stories as property.
- **Mythos:** Anansi, who tricked the sky god out of all stories and considers the sale final. The empire assembled in under six years through acquisitions nobody can reconstruct — the mythic tell.
- **Motive:** The Flats is the one district she can't own — its stories are oral. Renewal burns the library. Vera was committing organized, publishable theft: *writing it down.* Her empire inherited the nickname-joke with the column that coined it; she renewed the joke's lease, then made the joke a body. Both were editorial decisions.
- **Method:** Never her hands, never her name. A true anecdote over drinks; Fitch understood. Hiding Vera's significance from Maas was deliberate — a routine docket entry creates no witness with leverage.
- **Play note:** She never lies. Every question gets a true story that leads elsewhere. Interviews feel like winning until the transcript is read back.

### Hal Rimm — Heimdall (the witness who won't)
- **Mundane:** Freelance "sound engineer" above the telephone Exchange, window on the Span. Early thirties and reads older — insomniac shadows, black hair combed back, two gold teeth from a jaw broken once; never seen sleeping. Taps across the city; everything worth keeping goes to reel-to-reel in the locked Hoard.
- **Mythos:** Heimdall. Records everything, testifies to nothing; the horn is blown once, at the end. His threshold: "when the bridge opens."
- **Holdings:** the ordering call (a laundered voice arranging the line item); years of the disappeared district on tape — the only recordings of erased people.
- **Motive:** he's watched his map go dark for months — Flats exchanges thinning call by call, the district growing quiet on tape. He knows what spreading silence on a switchboard means.
- **Hidden hand:** the tip that put the PCs' agency on the case was routed through an untraceable Exchange line. Arranging witnesses isn't testifying. Payoff line when met: *"Who do you think told your agency there was something worth seeing?"* (Silently droppable if unused.)

### Geraldine "Gerry" Fitch — the second hand (no Mythos)
- Maas's gopher and contracts officer; Maas calls her "my girl" and believes she's loyal. She's Weaver's — recruited about a year ago when Viv learned her first name and asked her opinion; the money matters, being *seen* matters more. A believer: Weaver is the future, and betraying Maas is backing the winner.
- Drafted the abatement line item naming Vera; forged the routing to look Authority-internal.
- **Under pressure she doesn't fold — she gets ambitious.** Tries to manage the investigation on Weaver's behalf unasked; that overreach is what exposes her. Cracks only when shown proof Weaver considers her disposable (Weaver inevitably does).
- The one satisfying, arrestable neck in the kill chain.

### Toby Small — the next mark
- **Mundane:** Fourteen, Puerto Rican on his mother's side ("Small" is his father's name), bilingual — runs words between the tower's English and the stoops' Spanish. Paperboy for Weaver's own morning edition; off the clock, cranked Vera's mimeograph and ran her bundles. Knows every stoop and shortcut. The party should know him early and casually.
- **Mythos:** Ratatoskr — the squirrel who runs the world-tree carrying messages between crown and roots. A paperboy, literally. Rat-named, which the Piper's ledger will find on-theme.
- **The noose:** humming a tune learned "off the sax man doing the relocation knocks." His block is on the docket; his date is the eve of its demolition — currently ~Day 18–20, **and the enemy can move it up.** He can restart *The Plain Truth* — loose end and heir at once.
- **Design note:** don't reveal he can name the sax man until the party learns the tune matters.

### August Klein — the pressman
- **Mundane:** Deaf since a boiler blast; runs the print shop below Vera's rooms; set every issue of *The Plain Truth*. Writes his half of conversations on a butcher-paper roll.
- **Mythos:** The Golem of Prague — the quarter's silent protector, animated by a written word. He runs on text, not sound; the song has no door into him. His *emet* is physically somewhere (the roll, a type slug in his breast pocket).
- **Holdings:** Vera's tune notation in her own invented system; the half-cut stencil; an old, laughed-off report of a prior river-walk he *saw*.
- **Escalation:** if the archive burns or Toby is taken, the Flats' defender wakes. The party learns why you don't destroy the words that animate a golem.

### Dez Okafor — the barber (suspect ring)
- **Mundane:** The Flats' barber and living library. Every family, feud, and disappearance, kept oral, recited only to the trusted.
- **Mythos:** A Griot. Third position in the theme war: Weaver *owns* stories, Vera *wrote* them, Dez *speaks* them.
- **Suspicion:** two public fallings-out with Vera. Six weeks out: he refused to give her the disappeared families' stories — the griot won't hand the living word to paper. Days before her death: she hummed him the tune; he recognized it, went gray, threw her out. Witnesses conflate the two fights into one grudge. Cold motive on the surface, richest source in the arc once earned. Knows the SCHULD lore.

### Mickey Shale — the columnist (suspect ring)
- **Mundane:** The youngest columnist the City ever had — inherited old Emmett Roan's column at twenty-eight and made his name *reviving* Roan's nickname for Vera, sharper and crueler. Washed up by his mid-forties, drinking at the Half Note.
- **Mythos:** A Judas — the betrayer whose payment never spends clean.
- **Suspicion:** had been meeting Vera quietly for three months before her death. Truth: the meetings were his idea — a drunk apology she coldly converted into Weaver Building access. She let the man who ruined her buy the coffee. His Day −14 slip at the paper is what killed her, and he doesn't know it yet. When he finds out is a scene to plan.

### "Queen" Ottilie Mauser — Tin City (suspect ring)
- **Mundane:** Boss of the underpass encampment; born in a Hoover camp, running camps since she could count. Early thirties, proud, prickly, protective.
- **Mythos:** The Mouse Queen — seven-crowned vermin royalty from the same folk soil as the Piper (she wears the seven crowns as medallions on a chain); his natural adversary.
- **Suspicion:** screamed at Vera in the street for photographing her people. Truth: she alone noticed that *documented* tenants are the ones who vanish. Keeps her own census in a coffee can — the irony being that her numbers are evidence too. She's fought the Piper for years without knowing his name. Her people include the canal witness.

### Rev. Eli Prosper — the mission (no Mythos)
- Black pastor of the Flats' largest congregation. Took Authority "relocation grants" to encourage buyouts. Vera had his canceled checks. Compromised, terrified, motive-shaped; ultimately a weak man. Confessor who cracks; proof not every wolf is a wolf.

### Mabel Cho — the switchboard (no Mythos, Vera's friend)
- Fed Vera tips from overheard calls for years. Grieving, scared, inside the Exchange — the human way toward Rimm. Testifies to Vera's last week: *distracted; humming.* Can place Fitch's upstairs calls.

## Key Locations

- **The Flats (Carver Flats)** — condemned tenement district in the expressway's path. **Who lives here:** Black and Puerto Rican blocks at the core — the Great Migration and the island a generation back — threaded with Jewish shopkeepers (Klein, Grosz) and aging German and Irish holdouts who never followed their children out. Two languages on the stoops, three in the market. Stoops, barbershop, mission, laundry lines. Oral-memory district; that's what makes it prey — and, in 1962, exactly the demographics renewal actually targeted, which makes the Authority's all-white org chart legible as part of the machine. Under the on-ramp: **Tin City** — the district's overflow plus Black veterans the GI Bill never reached.
- **Klein's print shop / Vera's rooms** — shop below, apartment above, back stairs between. **The Archive:** card files by name and address, carbons of every records request, photos of tenants the City says never lived there, condemnation notices with names penciled on backs. One drawer empty — *Vera* emptied it (Day −3) and hid the contents.
- **The Weaver Building** — paper, radio, Channel 6 stacked; rooftop garden threads visible from the street. Studio B: where the nickname happened. The clippings morgue has Flats-shaped gaps — and a log of Vera's photo request.
- **The Renewal Authority / Model Room** — gilt scale model of the future City, Flats already a golden plaza. The model runs *ahead of reality.* Signing desk: where the docket dies.
- **The Exchange** — switchboard fortress. Upstairs behind LINE MAINTENANCE: Rimm's listening room and the Hoard.
- **The river / Pier 9** — the walk-in point, this time with a body. Prior pattern reads as folded-clothes suicides, no remains — each dated to the eve of a block clearance, and coming faster every month.
- **The Half Note** — after-hours room; Bunting's Thursday chair; the arc's only live music. Where Vera was infected (Day −10) while surveilling him. Shale drinks here.
- **The Span** — unfinished elevated expressway dead-ending in air over the Flats. Rimm's window faces it. Ribbon-cutting is the campaign's visible clock.

## Background — Motives First

### Years back — the standing arrangements
- **Weaver** converts the City's voice into property with unaccountable speed: silk-fortune money becomes paper → radio license → Channel 6 in under six years, through deals nobody can quite reconstruct. The Flats never sells — its memory lives out loud, unownable. A gap in the collection; gaps offend a collector. (The nickname-joke predates her — she bought the column that coined it, and renewed the lease.)
- **Maas** rises on the Authority. His projects gleam and empty. Two years ago his daughter saw a clearance ledger she wasn't meant to and left without explaining; he files it under ingratitude.
- **Bunting** is robbed on schedule his whole playing life — the credited-away solo, the royalties, the pension — while the Rift settles into him. Early collections are small and deniable.
- Early in the renewal push, **Weaver mentions to Maas** — on air, off record, at one of her salons — that she knows a man who can empty a building without a marshal or a headline. The two legends recognize each other and say nothing about it.
- **Vera**, age eight, tells the truth on a live microphone; the City laughs for thirty years.
- **Rimm** marries his equipment into the Exchange line by line, and keeps everything.
- **August** sees a man stroll into the river ~18 months ago, reports it in writing, is laughed out of the precinct.

### Six months out — the Span changes everything
- Approach-ramp route finalized: it lands on the Flats' core blocks. Ribbon-cutting goes on calendars. This is the accelerant for every motive below.
- **Maas** gets a deadline and a legacy; signs expanded condemnation dockets knowing what the abatement line does, repeating "relocation" until he can sleep.
- **Weaver's** pages begin the blight drumbeat — the district narrated toward its own erasure, an obituary in installments.
- **Bunting's** work goes steady for the first time since the session years — a collection every few weeks, then biweekly as the docket thickens. The books are beautiful.
- **Prosper** takes his first relocation grant and preaches acceptance at a manageable self-hatred.
- **Ottilie** notices Tin City's arithmetic is wrong — arrivals exceed departures, the camp shrinks — and starts a private census in a coffee can.
- **The tempo:** vanishings ran one every month or two for years — scattered, deniable. The Span schedule compresses everything: monthly becomes biweekly becomes, in the last three weeks, *weekly*. The acceleration is what finally makes the pattern findable — and what makes the next one always days away, not weeks.

### Three months out — the pattern finds its finder
- **Vera** connects the folded-clothes suicides to the clearance map. Correlation only, no myth yet. The archive turns from corruption to *people*.
- **Mabel's** year of overheard-call tips finally has somewhere to land.
- **Shale** starts meeting Vera — his idea, a drunk apology she coldly converts into Weaver Building access. She lets him buy the coffee.
- **Fitch**, Weaver's for about a year (Viv learned her first name and asked her opinion — the whole recruitment), passes docket schedules upstream and begins believing she's a principal, not a courier.
- **Toby** graduates to mimeograph crank; Vera pays in sandwiches and proofreading, training a successor without noticing.

### Six weeks out — everyone in motion
- **Vera** finds the correlation that the *acceleration* finally made visible: every folded-clothes "suicide" lands on the eve of a block clearance — and there have been three in the last three weeks. Files the photo request at the Weaver morgue — her name now in the one building where names are inventory.
- She asks **Dez** for the disappeared families' stories; he refuses flat — the griot won't hand the living word to paper. First public friction (the second, over the tune, comes later).
- **Rimm** watches his map go dark — Flats exchanges thinning call by call — and pulls reels into a separate rack. He knows what spreading silence on a switchboard means. Testimony is forbidden; *arrangement*, he begins to consider, may not be.

### The last month — convergence
- Final docket posts; Toby's block is on it. Bunting scouts it and keeps tipping the kid his morning dime.
- **Vera** closes on the "relocation contractor" as one person — work orders, a description, a horn case — perhaps ten days from a name.
- **Shale's slip** (Day −14): gin-warm, he tells his editor he's helping Little Miss ___ with something big. Weaver hears by lunch, checks the morgue request log, and understands in one sitting: she's assembling the district's memory into publishable form. Theft of the only property he considers real.
- The anecdote over drinks → Fitch drafts → Maas signs the docket on the 12th without reading page 31, which changes nothing about what he knew the docket was for → Bunting gets a name, an address, a deadline — and on Thursday the target walks into his club on her own feet.
- Then her week, as below.

## The Crime Scene (Node 0) — Full Inventory

**Situation on arrival:** just before dawn. Cordon up, a handful of uniforms keeping distance from the team — word has come down that this one belongs to the agency. The ME's roving lab van is parked at the pier head, generator running. Across the canal, the Renewal billboard catches the last of the moon.

### Environment
- **Pier 9 and the shoreline.** Creosote, river mud, graffiti on the pilings. Tide was slack around 2 a.m.
- **Footprints:** one set, bare, woman's size, starting exactly where the shoes sit. Even stride, no hesitation marks, no turning. They walk *in* like someone crossing a room.
- **No second set of prints on the mud** — the shooter never left the planked pier. On the pier itself: scuff marks at the rail ~40 ft from her entry point, where someone stood a long while. A waxed-paper peppermint wrapper ground under a heel (Bunting's tell — horn player, keeps his mouth clean; recurs wherever he waits).
- **Ballistics at scene:** no brass anywhere (revolver — nothing ejected). The miss is buried in a piling: .38, pristine rifling, recoverable if someone thinks to look for a third shot. (The witness heard three; only two wounds.)
- **Shooter geometry:** backlit by the streetlight at the pier head — which is why the witness across the canal saw only a silhouette, and why the silhouette never saw the witness.
- **The impossibility:** she was recovered *upstream* of her entry, resting in the grain-elevator eddy against the current. Harbor patrol will say, quietly, that the river doesn't do that.

### The Body
- Late thirties. Hair pinned for a workday. Glasses still on her face, unbroken, lenses thick — she could barely see without them. A cheap pencil still tucked behind her ear, through everything.
- **Wounds:** entry high left shoulder from behind, through-and-through (slug lost to the water). Entry back of the skull — the kill — slug recovered by the ME, deformed. Angles consistent with a shooter on the elevated pier behind her. No defensive wounds of any kind.
- **Hands:** chronic mimeograph-ink stains, a crank-handle callus on the right palm, stencil wax under two fingernails.
- **The lungs (why the ME keeps revising):** the head wound is instantly fatal — yet there is aspirated river water. Both cannot be first. The sequence refuses to settle, and Dr. Halloran knows it.
- **Time of death:** consistent with the witness's 2 a.m. Her wristwatch is cheap and still running.

### Clothing
- Coat and shoes **folded and squared** shoreside, stockings folded inside the shoes. The fold is precise, unhurried — and it matches, exactly, the eleven prior clothes-piles in the harbor unit's memory and the ME's paperwork. Nobody has said this out loud yet.
- She's in her hearing-attendance dress — what Mabel will call her *fighting clothes*. She dressed for something.

### Items on the Body
- **Cigarettes:** a pack of Chesterfields, one cigarette flipped (her lucky). Folded into the cellophane, a note in her handwriting: the top two-thirds is one word written over and over, increasingly shaky — **SCHULD** — *a word she had no reason to know.* Beneath it, one lucid line: **"AK — check the roll."** (August Klein; the butcher-paper roll; the notation.)
- **Matchbook** from the Half Note (places her there — Day −10 corroboration).
- **Key ring:** apartment, shop backdoor, and one unmarked key nobody can match to a lock (the hidden card-drawer cache — long-fuse clue).
- **A dime** loose in the coat pocket, plus the torn stencil corner and the smell of mimeo ink.
- **From the lab van:** tox screen clean except ~one shot of rye taken around midnight. (The bottle and a single glass are on her kitchen table at home — she had one drink, alone, before the walk. Whether that was ritual, courage, or the last human thing she did is for the table to argue about.)

### The Gun (what happened to it)
- Not at the scene — **as of this morning it is still on Bunting's person** (coat pocket, then a coffee can in his room). He hates having it; it's an unbalanced line in his books.
- **His plan:** once he feels clear — day 3 or 4 — he'll do the bookkeeper thing: **return it to the pawnshop he bought it from** (Solly Grosz, Three Balls Loans) and recover most of his money. Bought, and eventually returned, under the alias **"M. Roland."** Cleaned; no prints.
- **The intercept window:** piling slug → rifling → make/model → pawn-ledger canvass. Fast players reach Grosz *before* the return and can be waiting, or flag Grosz to call — catching Bunting with the murder weapon in a paper bag. Slow players get the ledger after the fact: Grosz remembers the polite man in the two-tone suit who *returned a revolver* — who does that? — with dates bracketing the murder. Either way it lands; speed decides how well. (Roland: another man famous for a horn. If a player catches the alias joke, let it land.)

### Witnesses
- **Primary — Elmore "Cutty" Sloan,** Black Korea veteran, Tin City, fishes nights under the canal side. Saw the whole thing across the water: heard her humming as she walked ("pretty, like off a record"), watched her stop at the waterline, stand two minutes, stop humming, and *speak* — he'll swear to the words: **"There's no other side. It's just water."** Then the silhouette at the rail: a hat, something long set down beside it — a glint off a case — then three flat cracks. She went in; she did not come up. **Rule for the table: Cutty can hum a bar of what he heard, and it is inert.** Secondhand carriage does not plant the seed; only the Piper's own delivery does. (Toby was seeded directly.)
- Cutty's problem: a vagrancy sheet and gin on his breath at 2 a.m. Nobody official has taken his statement seriously. He is also one of Ottilie's, and will not talk twice without her nod.
- **Tertiary:**
  - **Night baker, canal side** — heard three flat cracks ~2 a.m., logged it mentally as a truck backfire. Corroborates time independent of Cutty.
  - **A tenant on the walk route** — saw her pass under the streetlight ~1:40, "walking like Sunday." Corroborates route and gait.
  - **Earl Dubcek, harbor patrol** — found her at 5:50 in the eddy. Thirty years on the water; keeps his own count: *"Eleven piles of clothes, no floaters. Till her."* Knows the river's currents well enough to say what her position means, if asked plainly.

### The Marks (supernatural)
- **The tongue:** the word **SCHULD** — but it isn't a tattoo. No pigment. Under the ME's glass it reads as *scar tissue in the shape of set type* — debossed, like a letterpress strike, in an old blackletter face. August will identify the face on sight: **Fraktur**, pre-war foundry type; he may even own the case it came from. The strike reads correctly to someone *facing her* — it was printed for the reader, not the bearer. Translation (Dez, or any old-country source): *debt* and *guilt* — one word for both.
- **The eyes:** solid black, sclera and iris both. No chemistry explains it. Every photograph fogs at the eyes only — the film comes back ruined in two neat circles. They cannot be documented, only looked into. (Gated: the right Mythos, or Dez's rite → the last image — a silhouette at a rail, a slim case, a streetlight behind.)
- **ME's official position on both:** "postmortem artifact," written with a question mark she is not proud of.

### Police at the Scene (names to remember)
- **Det. Frank Casale, Homicide** — nominal case officer, territorial for about ninety seconds and then visibly relieved: he's smart enough to smell career poison. Will trade. **Big picture if asked right:** knows the "jumper piles" exist as paperwork — folded clothes, no body, closed as suicides — and can pull the files if given a reason that protects him.
- **Sgt. Roy Pruitt** — uniform running the cordon. On the Authority's quiet stipend list. Hovers, notes what the team examines, offers gentle steers ("she was a known crank, you know"). After the team leaves, he makes a call from the corner box. Catching him at it is the players' first thread into the stipend list.
- **Officers Stan Kowalczyk & Danny Boyd, car 6** — the cold-open cruiser. Boyd, young, is bothered by the disregard order and will say so over coffee; Kowalczyk, twenty years in, tells him to drop it, which tells the players more than Boyd does.
- **Dr. Ruth Halloran, ME** — chain-smoking, exact, allergic to mystery in her own paperwork. Runs the van. **Big picture if asked right:** she has signed off on the clothes-pile suicides for four years — nine files across her desk — and has a private memo about them she's never sent. She has *not* correlated the dates. A player who asks "what dates?" hands her the worst afternoon of her career.
- **Desk Sgt. Aldo Brack** (not at scene) — took Cutty's 2:15 a.m. call and logged it as a drunk-and-disorderly caller, low priority. Defensive about it in exact proportion to how wrong it was.
- **Lorna Feeny, dispatch** (not at scene) — was on the board when the disregard came down; heard the muffled voice behind the order. A name for later.

### Why the Body Sat Until Dawn (the delay, fully accounted)
1. **2:15 a.m.** — Cutty reaches a call box. Desk Sgt. Brack hears gin and a vagrancy-sheet voice, logs it low priority.
2. **~2:40 a.m.** — Car 6 finally assigned to swing by.
3. **~2:47 a.m.** — The disregard: dispatch pulls car 6 mid-route. Surface truth: the agency's strings, securing the scene for the right hands. Buried truth: the tip that moved those strings originated from an untraceable Exchange line hours *before* any of this was official — Rimm was ahead of the paperwork.
4. **5:50 a.m.** — Harbor patrol's routine dawn run finds her in the eddy. Nobody was looking; the river simply presented her.
- The gap has three fingerprints on it — Brack's prejudice, the disregard order, harbor routine — and each is separately investigable. The delay isn't sloppiness; it's the first exhibit.

## The Gates

Design rules: every resolution needs its findings **in concert** (no single-clue shortcuts); every finding has 2–3 independent sources; every tier reachable by ≥2 paths; order-flexible.

### Tier 1 — PIPER: identity + method + mandate
- **Identity** (any 2): pawnshop ledger • Ottilie's people describing the relocation man • Half Note staff placing Vera watching a sax man, Day −10 • black-eyes vision.
- **Method** (any 2): the notation on Klein's roll • Dez's reaction to the hummed tune / SCHULD lore • Toby humming the same tune (arms the rescue clock) • witness detail: she hummed while walking calm.
- **Mandate** (any 1): the abatement work order • Bunting's own ledger • Fitch's routing stamp on anything.
- **Resolution:** who, how, and that he was *hired* — forcing the next question. Without mandate, they've found a man they can't arrest or explain.

### Tier 2 — MAAS: signature + knowledge + pattern
- **Signature** (any 1): the signed docket • clerk testimony on his signing routine. Damning, not exculpatory — he knows what the docket is.
- **Knowledge** (any 2): years of "abatement/relocation" line items with no receiving records anywhere • his private budget notes pricing removals above any legitimate service • Ida's account of why she left • Ottilie: Authority men watched a clearance happen.
- **Pattern** (any 2): vanishings mapped to demolition eves on the dockets • folded-clothes suicides on cleared blocks, frequency rising with the Span schedule • the Model Room running ahead of reality.
- **Resolution:** the machine is policy and Maas runs it knowingly. *And* one line item didn't originate inside the Authority — someone used the machine for a private kill. → Fitch → Tier 3.
- **The flip:** betrayal, not guilt. Proof that Fitch is Weaver's and Vera was Weaver's hit through Maas's signature turns him — pride and control, cooperating only in ways that protect the machine.

### Tier 3 — WEAVER: the hand + the motive + the voice
- **The hand** (any 2): Fitch's payments traced to a Weaver subsidiary • Shale's confession of the Day −14 slip • Ida Maas's documents • Fitch cracked (only via proof Weaver finds her disposable).
- **The motive** (any 2): Vera's photo request logged in the Weaver morgue • the recovered card-drawer file • the morgue's Flats-shaped gaps.
- **The voice** (any 1, hard-won): Rimm's reel of the ordering call • Weaver's own slip — a trickster who cannot lie, playing interview games on her own program.
- **Resolution:** intent, authorship, near-proof — and the problem stops being epistemic. The finale is what to *do* with it: Weaver signed nothing. Making the story point at the spider is the endgame (debt-reversal: prove the welch, let the legend collect from the true debtor).

## The Next Moves — Reactive Schedules & Clocks

What each figure does if the players never interfere. Player pressure shifts these; wasted time lets them complete.

### The Clocks (player-facing urgency)
1. **The Inquest — ~Day 10.** Official ruling: suicide. Closes the police file, cuts evidence access, buries Halloran's doubts — unless challenged or delayed. The bureaucratic clock.
2. **The Condemnation Hearing — ~Day 14.** Toby's block confirmed for clearance. Public stage: Maas presiding, Fitch at his elbow, press (Weaver's) in the row. Players can attend, disrupt, or work the room.
3. **Toby's Demolition Eve — ~Day 18–20 at first posting.** The rescue clock — and it is *movable*: the hearing (Clock 2) or Fitch's counterplay can pull it earlier. The players should watch the demolition schedule the way sailors watch weather.
4. **The Ribbon-Cutting — ~Day 37.** The Span opens. Maas's triumph, Weaver's front page, Rimm's horn threshold, and — if unresolved — the district's practical end.
5. **The Next Pile — Day 2–3.** A holdout block on the docket clears; someone from Vera's archive — a name the players may have just read on a card — leaves folded clothes on a shore while the investigation is barely started. The pattern is not historical; it is *running.*
6. *Soft:* **the gun return — Day 3–4.** Bunting visits Grosz. Intercept window.
7. *Soft:* **Vera's letter — Day 4–5.** Arrives (Shale, or general delivery). Its holder sits on it, scared.

### Bunting
- **Day 1:** routine as camouflage — coffee, paper, the morning knocks. Reads Weaver's own paper at noon: *body recovered.* First one ever. He knows precisely what that means: an unsettled entry. The gun stays in the coat because throwing away money isn't in him.
- **Days 2–3:** works. A holdout block clears on schedule and he attends its eve — the Next Pile (Clock 5). Scouts Toby's block per the docket, tips the kid his dime. Sleeps badly. Thursday: plays flat; regulars notice.
- **Day 3–4:** the pawnshop return (see Gun).
- **Week 2:** if the stencil, notation, or "relocation man" talk surfaces publicly, he visits Klein's shop — professionally curious, nothing planted, just *looking*. A quiet, terrifying scene if players are present.
- **Week 2 onward:** mythic pressure to settle Vera's entry grows. Completion logic: collect what she loved most — **the archive.** An attempt on the shop (fire or theft) is his make-good — and the event that can wake August. Then Toby's block, on its eve.
- **If cornered early:** he doesn't run. He negotiates — collections are negotiable, that's the whole point of him. What he asks for is what he's owed, and no one can pay it.

### Weaver
- **Day 1:** the story gets shaped: page-7 drowning, "local character," obituary as final editorial insult; on the afternoon program, a light joke in the monologue. Follow-up coverage dies in the morning meeting. Nothing written, nothing ordered.
- **Days 2–4:** learns through channels (Pruitt → Fitch) that an agency, not Homicide, holds the case. Response: a friendly feature writer starts asking who these investigators are. She collects people before opposing them.
- **Week 2:** if the players are effective: a gracious invitation — a salon, or a segment on the program — trickster games on home ground, on camera. Possibly a flattering profile of the agency (owning their story is the threat).
- **Weeks 3–4:** if genuinely pressed: narrative violence, never physical — Cutty smeared in print, Halloran's competence questioned, the agency's mandate challenged at city hall. If Fitch has overreached by now, Weaver begins quietly treating her as disposable — which is the crack that turns Fitch.
- **Ribbon-cutting:** her front page, her cameras, her finished obituary of the Flats — broadcast live, hosted by her.

### Maas
- **Day 1:** nothing. A drowned crank does not reach his desk. He signs things.
- **Week 2:** the condemnation hearing — his stage, his golden model, his sincere speech about the future.
- **If the pattern surfaces publicly:** Authority lawyers, an internal "audit" — which Fitch runs, using it to purge and plant. Maas believes the audit is real. That gap between what he ordered and what Fitch executes is evidence, if players can catch the delta.
- **The flip window:** only after Tier 2 — and only via the betrayal wedge. Before that, he is a wall of reasonable, golden certainty.

### Fitch
- **Days 1–3:** monitors through Pruitt; reports upstream in anecdotes, Weaver-style; she's learning the master's grammar.
- **Week 1–2:** **the overreach** — inserts herself as the Authority's "liaison" to the investigation, offers helpfully sanitized records, suggests avenues. Every helpful gesture deposits fingerprints.
- **If squeezed:** doesn't fold — escalates. Arranges obstruction through Brack/Pruitt, and in the dangerous branch, **warns Bunting** (she holds the contractor file; she knows exactly who the relocation man is). This accelerates every Bunting clock.
- **Cracks only** when shown proof Weaver considers her disposable.

### Rimm
- **Day 1:** pulls last night's reels — the call box, the dispatch board, the disregard. Files them in the separate rack. Waits.
- **Ongoing:** the Hoard grows. Approaches bounce off; the door doesn't open without Mabel, or without the players proving they matter to his threshold.
- **His clock is #4.** As the ribbon-cutting nears, he becomes *more* available, not less — a watchman preparing for the one permitted act.

### Toby
- **Day 1:** grieving loudly at the cordon's edge; attaches himself to the party — papers, errands, nickels. Humming.
- **Weeks 1–2:** the humming persists, harmless-seeming. (The seed lies quiet until its date approaches.)
- **Final week:** symptoms — distraction, the tune constant, and one night he's found standing at the canal rail, confused about how he got there. The near-miss is the alarm bell if the players haven't connected the humming.
- **Demolition eve, ~2 a.m.:** the walk, unless prevented.

### Shale
- **Day 1:** writes the honest obituary; his editor spikes it; the cruel one runs. He drinks accordingly.
- **Days 4–5:** Vera's letter reaches him (if he's the recipient). He sits on it — opening it means admitting what he already suspects about his slip.
- **Week 2–3:** learns, or deduces, that his barroom brag is what killed her. His Judas legend pulls hard toward self-destruction; whether the players reach him first — with the letter, with work to do, with the chance to testify against Weaver — decides whether he ends as a source or a casualty.

### August
- **Day 1:** shop closed — first time anyone remembers. He knows something is owed the moment he hears; the roll has her handwriting on it.
- **On contact:** shown the cigarette-pack note ("AK — check the roll"), he produces everything, in writing, methodically. Full cooperation.
- **If the archive is attacked** (Bunting's completion, Fitch's audit reaching too far, Prosper's fumbling): the golem wakes. This should be sudden, enormous, and *protective* — and it exposes his emet to anyone watching closely.

### Ottilie
- **Day 1:** Tin City locks down. Cutty is unreachable without Ottilie's nod.
- **Respected:** the coffee-can census, the witness, years of pattern. **Raided or condescended to:** a wall, permanently.
- **Risk branch:** if players reveal too much about the relocation man, Ottilie moves on her own — the Mouse Queen marching on the Piper. Mice against the piper is a massacre; this can cost Cutty, or Ottilie herself, and teach the players that information is ammunition someone else can fire.

### Halloran
- **Days 1–3:** revisions, the question-mark report, professional misery.
- **If a player prompts the date correlation:** she runs it, goes quiet, and becomes an ally-at-risk — pressure from upstairs to sign "suicide" before the inquest, her private memo suddenly a liability.
- **The inquest (~Day 10)** is her deadline as much as the players': what she testifies to depends on what they've given her the professional cover to say.

### Prosper
- **Week 1:** hears the archive survived Vera. The canceled checks with his endorsement are in there somewhere.
- **~Day 6–8:** the amateur break-in at Klein's — clumsy, frightened, looking only for his own sins. **Deliberate red herring:** evidence of intrusion at the shop mid-arc, pointing every player instinct at the enemy, resolving as a weak man protecting himself. Costs the players time if they spend it; humanizes the board either way.

### Mabel
- **Days 1–3:** grief, fear, and a decision forming. If the players seem trustworthy, she reaches out on her own by end of week one — she has a year of overheard calls and nobody left to give them to.
- **At risk from Day 10+:** if Fitch senses the Exchange leaking, her job — and the players' route to Rimm — is what he squeezes.

### Pruitt & Brack
- **Pruitt:** reports the players' movements nightly from the corner box. Catching him is cheap, early, and rich — the stipend list is the first paper connecting the Authority's money to the police.
- **Brack:** digs in on the downgraded call in exact proportion to how wrong it was. Boyd, over coffee, is the soft route around him.

## The Decoy Layer (misdirection per tier)

Full profiles in the roster doc. The principle: each decoy moves *independently* of player attention, is guilty of a real lesser crime, and converts to a resource after deflation.

- **Tier 1 — Sal "Big Bad" Lupo** (demolition/eviction muscle, Big Bad Wolf): hunting the Piper himself for business reasons; armed, violent, photographed by Vera, keeps surfacing where the clues point. Deflates on method (he leaves marks; the walks leave none). Converts to: clearance job-logs + a blunt weapon. Danger branch: he finds Bunting first.
- **Tier 1–2 — Ray Sato** (ex-cop Vera exposed, now her secret convert): surveillance photos of the victim, gun, grudge, no alibi. Deflates on the photos themselves — his Day −10 roll contains a long-lens frame of the Half Note with a two-tone suit at the edge. Converts to: a trained parallel investigator with a debt to the dead.
- **Tier 1–2 — Nathaniel Pell** (Beacon Mutual's Flats agent): eleven claim files on the folded-clothes deaths, a public fight with Vera over the denial records three weeks before her death, and a habit of appearing wherever death does. Deflates on cowardice — he knew and monetized silence, nothing more. Converts to: the actuarial spine of the pattern (a Tier 2 pattern source independent of Halloran) plus the families' trust as a door-opener.
- **Tier 2 — Councilwoman Maeve Brogan** (widow's-seat ward boss): obstructs the moment the investigation nears the Authority, because her own graft is adjacent. Cover-up reads as conspiracy at exactly the "who ordered it" tier. Deflates on the money (kickbacks, not contracts-for-killing). Converts to: procedural power — inquest delays, hearing schedules, and a grudge against Maas.
- **Tier 2–3 — Greta Milner** (Spindle & Milner Credit, Rumpelstiltskin): the *visible* money above Maas — liens maturing on condemnation, a loan dressing Fitch, contracts cruel and legal to the letter. Deflates on the signature principle: her name is on everything, and the author's is on nothing — the anti-Viv, and the lesson in how to recognize the real spider. Converts to: the financial map, including a payment chain into a subsidiary she once failed to reconstruct herself — the visible edge of Viv's web.
- **Tier 3 — Preston Day** (radio's fading sun-king, Apollo): the *visible* media suspect — transmitter tower on cleared Flats land, a Vera file, on-air "dealt with" remarks on tape. Deflates on the truth-god streak: he has never knowingly lied on air, verifiably. Converts to: the one microphone Viv doesn't own — the finale's broadcast platform.

Timing: introduce Lupo and Sato in week one (both are already in motion around the case); Brogan activates when the Authority is touched; Day surfaces the first time a player says "follow the media."
