# Leads

Read-aloud clue blocks at the follow-up locations. Each is one thing to look at, written to be read as is, followed by an italic GM line: what it's about, and whether it's a true lead, a **false lead** toward a suspect, or **background** on Vera, the district, or the renewal. Generic location text is in [[Read-Aloud]]; the pier is in [[Crime Scene]]; the clue web is in [[The Case]].

---

## Klein Job Printing

#### The roll
> A yard of butcher paper pulled off the spindle and pinned flat with a type slug at each corner. Block capitals in one hand, and in another hand, smaller, slanting, a line of marks that aren't letters: dots and dashes and a few little arrows, in groups of four, over and over down the margin, with the word *TUNE* written once and underlined twice.
*Vera's notation for the four notes, in a system she invented. The last line on the roll, in her hand, dated Day 0, 4 p.m., is a location hint for the hidden file (decide at table). True lead: Okafor can read the arrows.*

#### The stencil
> On the mimeograph drum, a stencil half cut, the wax scored in a typewriter's teeth and stopped mid-word. What's cut reads as a headline, THE NINTH, and under it a column that begins with a street name and a date and gets as far as the third name before the cutting stops.
*The disappeared-persons issue, unfinished. The three names are on the demolition dockets. True lead, and background: this is what she died for.*

#### The old report
> From a drawer under the counter: a carbon of a letter to the precinct, eighteen months old, in block capitals, describing a man in a coat walking into the river at Pier 9 at two in the morning "WITHOUT HURRY," and a reply clipped to it on precinct letterhead, two lines long, thanking the writer for his concern.
*Klein saw a walker and was laughed off. Background on the pattern; true lead that the precinct has known for a year and a half. Note the carbon: he wrote it by hand and it never went anywhere a wire could carry it, which is why it still exists.*

#### The card drawers
> Filing cabinets to the ceiling, every drawer labeled by street. Pull one: index cards, one per family, name, address, date of the notice, date they left, and where they went. On maybe one card in five the last line is blank. On maybe one in ten it says a street that isn't on the map on the wall.
*The archive. Background on the renewal; true lead on the forwarding addresses (Prosper) and the Next Pile (a name the players may read tonight).*

#### The empty drawer
> One drawer, the one labeled with the pier's street, pulls out light. Empty. Not dusty: the runners are clean and the drawer has been used this week. A pencil rolls to the front when you open it.
*Vera emptied it Day −3 and hid the contents. The unmarked key on her ring fits wherever they are. True lead, long fuse.*

#### The map
> A district map on the corkboard, and in it pins in three colors. The red ones cluster on the river blocks. The blue ones follow the red a month behind. The white ones are on blocks that aren't gone yet, and one of them is on the block outside this window.
*Red: a pile. Blue: the demolition that followed. White: dockets posted. Background on the pattern; the white pin on this block is Toby's.*

#### The kitchen table upstairs
> Oilcloth, one chair, one glass with a finger of rye still in it, one bottle with the cap off. The chair is pulled out and turned toward the window, and the window looks at the white fence.
*One drink alone before the walk. Background: what she did with her last evening.*

#### Vera's first issue
> Framed by the stairs, the only thing in the shop that's framed: a mimeographed sheet, purple gone gray, eleven years old, masthead hand-lettered. THE PLAIN TRUTH. PRINTED WEEKLY. RIGHT EVENTUALLY. The lead story is about a sewer contract.
*Background: who she was. Toby will tell you she paid same day.*

#### The clipping
> Taped inside a type-case drawer where nobody would look: a newspaper photograph gone brown, thirty years old, of a stage with a scale model on it and a row of men in suits, and at the edge of the stage, a small girl in a dress with thick glasses and her mouth open, and a caption that gives her a nickname and doesn't give her name.
*Background: the nickname's origin. Weaver's paper printed it. Vera kept it.*

#### After Lupo's crew
> Upstairs, drawers on the floor, cards everywhere like a dropped deck, the mattress cut open, the corkboard torn down. Nothing burned. Nothing taken that you can tell. Whoever did this was looking for something with a name on it and didn't find it, and wanted you to know they'd been.
*False lead toward Lupo: it looks like the killer came back. It's the Wolf hunting the same man.*

## Tin City

#### The can
> A coffee can on a crate, and in it, folded small, hundreds of strips of paper, each with a name and a date and one word: IN or OUT. The OUT strips have a second date. Some of them have a third word, and the third word is the same word every time: GONE.
*Ottilie's census. Nine years. True lead on the pattern, independent of the precinct.*

#### The tally
> Chalk on the concrete leg behind the chair, five-bar gates in rows, hundreds, and every so often one gate struck through with a diagonal in a different chalk. The struck ones have a mark beside them, the same mark every time: a little arrow, pointing down.
*Departures the camp couldn't account for. The arrow is Ottilie's, not Vera's, and it means the same thing.*

#### Cutty's crate
> A milk crate at the water's edge, a line tied to a spike, a bait can. From here the pier is thirty yards across the canal and the lamp on its bent post sits dead center, like a spotlight on a stage with nobody on it. You could see a hat at the rail from here. You could not see a face.
*Confirms the witness geometry. True lead.*

#### Where the rats were
> Along the base of the concrete legs, a runway worn smooth in the cinders, and burrows, dozens, all of them empty. Not a rat in the camp. Ottilie's people have stopped setting the traps and started leaving food out, and the food is still there.
*They left the night of. True lead on the rats; background on how the camp lives with them.*

## The Half Note

#### The hat
> On a hook behind the bar, among the umbrellas nobody claimed: a short-brim hat, good felt, old, the band sweated through and the crown dented on one side from years of the same hand. There's a peppermint in the band.
*Left Tuesday by the sax man. True lead: the wrapper's owner has a hat here.*

#### The stand
> A music stand at the end of the little stage with no music on it and a chair behind it that's been pushed back at an angle, the way a chair is left by someone who stood up in a hurry. On the floor under it, a reed, split.
*The Tuesday number he played wrong. True lead.*

#### The stage door
> The alley door, propped with a brick. Three rats on the step, sitting, not eating the bread somebody has put down for them. Under the step, a mousetrap, sprung, empty. Another one by the bins, the same.
*They wait where he'll be. True lead on the rats.*

#### The cigar
> In the ashtray at the end of the bar, one cigar butt, thick, expensive, chewed more than smoked. Nobody who drinks here smokes cigars.
*Lupo's foreman was in Day 0 asking about the sax player. False lead toward Lupo, and a true one: somebody else is hunting him.*

#### The wall of photos
> Over the bar, photographs of the room in better years, the band on the stand, the crowd. In one of them, eleven men on a stand twice this size, and at the end, half out of the frame, a saxophone player in a suit that's two colors.
*Background: he used to be somebody. True lead if anyone looks twice.*

## Lupo Brothers Wrecking & Salvage

#### The whiteboard
> A board in the shed office with streets and dates in grease pencil, and beside each date a dollar figure, and beside half the dollar figures a word in capitals: LOST. The dates run in order down the board and the LOSTs get closer together toward the bottom.
*Lupo's crews are losing clearance contracts to "the relocation man." Background on the machine; false lead (motive) and true lead (the schedule).*

#### The photograph
> Pinned to the shed wall with a nail through the middle: a newspaper photograph of two men carrying a sofa out of a doorway past a woman on the stoop with her hands over her face, and the caption names the company. Someone has drawn a circle around the photographer's credit line in the corner.
*Vera took it. False lead: it's the reason Lupo's men were on her block. He wanted the negatives, not her.*

## The Furnished Room (Sato)

#### The wall
> The whole wall. A map in the middle with the river blocks shaded, and around it, photographs on index cards, dozens, pinned and strung. The same small woman in thick glasses appears in most of them, walking, buying a paper, standing at a fence. Under each, a date in a hand that presses hard enough to dent the card. The last date is eleven days ago.
*False lead: it looks like a stalker's wall. It's a believer's. True lead: the dates and places are Vera's last week, accurate.*

#### The bandstand frame
> One photograph on the wall is different from the others: taken through a window at night, grainy, a bar with a stand at the far end and a woman alone at a table watching it. At the edge of the stand, almost out of frame, a man with a saxophone in a suit that photographs as two shades of gray.
*Day −10. The suit. True lead, and Sato doesn't know what he has.*

#### The other man
> In one frame, dated nineteen days ago, the woman in glasses is talking on a stoop to a man in a good coat with his hat brim low. He's writing in a notebook.
*Only if Erik accepted the offer that Vera hired Rook: it's Rook. Otherwise it's a process server, and a loose end.*

#### The gun
> On the dresser, a service revolver, cleaned, oiled, in a holster with the department's stamp still on the leather. Beside it, the back issues of a mimeographed weekly, every one, in order, with paragraphs underlined and notes in the margins in the same pressing hand.
*False lead: motive and means. True lead: he read every word she wrote and believed it.*

## The Mission

#### The checks
> In the desk drawer under a Bible, a bundle of canceled checks with a rubber band around them. The payer is the Authority. The memo line on every one says TRANSITION ASSISTANCE. The endorsements on the back are all the same signature, and the signature gets smaller down the pile.
*Prosper took money to preach acceptance. False lead: he's guilty, not of this. Background on how the machine bought the district.*

#### The list
> Inside the Bible's back cover, a sheet folded small: eleven names, eleven addresses in another part of the City, each in the Authority's typewriter face, and beside each, in pencil, a date and a word. The word is NOBODY, eleven times.
*The forwarding addresses. He visited every one. Calder Row doesn't exist. True lead on the pattern.*

#### The blank rectangles
> On the sanctuary wall, among the family photographs, spaces where photographs have been taken down: eleven pale rectangles in the paint, in a row, at eye height.
*He couldn't look at them. Background.*

## The Barbershop

#### The photographs
> Hundreds of them, no names. But the ones on the wall by the door are newer than the rest, and they're of the same families as the older ones on the far wall, and between the two walls, in the middle, there's a gap the size of a door where the tacks are still in the plaster and the photographs aren't.
*The vanished. Okafor took them down and keeps them in the back. He won't say so to strangers. Background; true lead once they're placed.*

#### The bench
> Three men on the bench who aren't waiting for haircuts. When the scissors stop, they look up. When the scissors start again, they don't.
*The district is listening. Whatever is said here is on the Flats by noon.*

## The 14th Precinct

#### The desk log
> A ledger open on the sergeant's counter, and eleven lines up from today's date: 2:15 AM, CANAL ST BOX, D&D CALLER, RE: PIER 9, NO CAR. The entry has been written over once to make it darker.
*Brack downgraded Cutty's call. True lead on the delay; the route to Tin City.*

#### The dispatch log
> In the closet with the board, the log book open to the night before last, and at 2:47, in a small quick hand, CAR 6 DISREGARD PER SHIFT SUPV, and in the margin, in pencil, so light you'd miss it, a time and a question mark.
*Feeny's note. True lead on the disregard; the voice with money in it.*

#### The nine files
> In a cabinet nobody has opened in a year, a run of folders with the same word stamped on each tab: JUMPER. Nine of them. Each has one page, a photograph of a pile of folded clothes on a shore, and a signature at the bottom in the same exact hand. No two photographs are of the same pier. All nine piles are folded the same way.
*Halloran signed them all. True lead on the pattern; Casale's price to pull them.*

## Grosz Loans & Appraisal

#### The ledger
> A page in the pawn ledger, three weeks back: REVOLVER, .38, S&W, one line, a price, and a name in the buyer's column in the same neat hand as everything else: M. ROLAND. No address. Two lines down the page, a watch, a trumpet, a wedding ring.
*The gun's purchase. True lead; the return comes Day 3–4 and gets its own line.*

#### The gun rack
> Behind the wire door, a row of pawned pistols with tags on the trigger guards. One tag is newer than the others, and the gun under it is cleaner than a pawned gun should be, and the tag says a name and a date that's this week.
*Only after Day 3–4. The returned .38. True lead: ballistics will match the piling.*

## The Exchange

#### The call slip
> A slip of switchboard paper, folded twice, passed under a cup: a date, 2:45 AM, an exchange number that belongs to the Authority's night line, connected to the precinct's shift supervisor, three minutes. In the corner, a set of initials that aren't the operator's.
*Mabel, end of week one. True lead on the disregard and the Authority. If they've earned more, she adds: calls coming in from numbers that were disconnected when the tenant vanished, with nobody on the line.*

## The Span Field Office

#### The whiteboard
> Bolted to the trailer wall, a grid of streets and dates in grease pencil. Some dates crossed out and rewritten. Every rewritten date is earlier than the one it replaced.
*The demolition schedule, accelerating. Background on the renewal; true lead on the clock. Toby's block is on it.*

#### The gate log
> A clipboard on a nail by the gate: vehicles in and out, by plate and time. The night before last, at 3:05 AM, an Authority car, in, and at 3:40, out. The guard's initials beside it are the same as on every other line.
*An Authority car at the pier before the cops. True lead; Padgett's price.*

## The Delancey Block, Fourth Floor

#### The drawer
> In the kitchen, a drawer with tea towels in it, and under the towels three envelopes with the Authority's seal. The first has been opened and read and refolded. The second has been opened and not unfolded. The third has not been opened at all.
*Herminia's three buyout letters. Background on the renewal; the next name.*

## The Weaver Building

#### Page seven
> Yesterday's morning edition, page seven, below the fold, four inches: LOCAL CHARACTER DROWNS. It uses the nickname in the first sentence and her name in the last. There is no byline.
*Weaver's paper shaped it, and the paragraph is the thread: this is how a soul rides into the wire. Background now; the mechanism later. Fitch made sure it ran on schedule.*

#### The spike
> On the city desk, a spike with a sheaf of copy on it, and near the top, six typed pages with a byline and a red grease-pencil line through the slug. The first sentence uses her name.
*Shale's honest obituary, killed by Herzog. True lead on Shale; background on Weaver's control.*
