# THE AGENCY — Player-Side Sketches

Deliberately lighter than the board — shape these around the PCs. The agency handles the cases that don't fit the mold; its people are the kind who stopped fitting molds themselves.

### Odessa Cole — Operations
**Concept:** The agency's spine. Former Army nurse — one of the units nobody made movies about — who ran forward surgical tents and now runs casework the same way: triage, worst bleeding first, feelings later. The one who pulled the strings that got the team the pier.

**Motivation (specific):** The agency's mandate survives on results and discretion, in that order, and she has buried two cases in her career that deserved daylight because the agency wouldn't have survived the exposure. Vera's case reads to her like a third — and she has privately decided she is not burying another one. The players will never hear this speech; they'll only notice she keeps finding them one more day, one more favor, one more piece of cover.

**Performable mannerisms:** Answers the phone mid-first-ring. Writes in a shorthand only she reads. States the time cost of everything ("That's a two-day favor you're asking for"). Has already thought of the thing you're about to say, and waits politely for you to finish saying it anyway.

**Voice:**
- [briefing]: "Homicide didn't want it, which is the first interesting thing about it. Second interesting thing's in the coroner's van. Go."
- [cover]: "You have until Thursday before somebody upstairs asks me why. Make me a liar by Wednesday."
- [the tell]: "I've closed cases that should've stayed open. Don't ask which. Just understand why this one's different, and don't waste it."

**Visual description:** Black woman, late forties, square-shouldered, close-pressed hair pinned back, a silk scarf knotted at the throat of a tailored jacket — the one soft thing on an otherwise operational silhouette. A small mole at the corner of her mouth; reading everything at a glance and filing it.

**Image prompt — headshot:** Bust-length view, cropped just below the collarbone, of a Black woman in her late forties with a strong oval face and a high forehead. Full, groomed, arched dark eyebrows; steady, wide-set dark brown eyes; a straight broad nose; a firm mouth with a small mole at one corner; dark hair pressed and pinned neatly back. Composed, command-level expression, facing the viewer. At the bottom edge of the frame only: a silk scarf knotted at the throat inside the collar of a tailored dark jacket.

**Image prompt — full body & wardrobe:** Full-figure view of a square-shouldered Black woman in her late forties standing straight with a folder held in one arm. She wears a tailored dark skirt suit with a silk scarf knotted at the throat, low sturdy heels, a plain wristwatch worn with the face on the inside of the wrist, and small pearl stud earrings.

### Esther Blum — Counsel
**Concept:** The agency's legal architecture, all of it, in one compact woman. Keeps the odd mandate alive through a lattice of retainers, favors, and precedents nobody else has bothered to read since 1911.

**Motivation (specific):** The law as a craft object — she genuinely loves the machinery, the way Klein loves type. What the renewal machine does to that craft (dockets as murder weapons, process as laundering) offends her the way a forged painting offends a restorer. Also, unstated and load-bearing: her family's building went under a "slum clearance" order in another city in another decade, and she has read every condemnation statute in this state twice since.

**Performable mannerisms:** Answers questions by asking the question you should have asked. Pushes her glasses up into her hair to think, forgets them there, owns four pairs for this reason. Cites case names like gossip ("Oh, that's a *Dowling* problem, and Dowling was a mess"). Cuts every meeting off one beat before it would have gone soft.

**Voice:**
- [advice]: "You can't subpoena a pawn ledger. You can, however, be standing beside it when someone opens it. Stand better."
- [the inquest]: "An inquest is a play with a script nobody admits exists. We can't rewrite it. We can recast it. Get me your coroner with her spine located."
- [the statutes]: "Condemnation law has exactly one honest sentence in it, and they put it in Latin so nobody would read it. I read it."

**Visual description:** Compact white woman, late fifties, silver-streaked dark curls barely contained by pins, reading glasses pushed up into them and forgotten. Sharp long nose, quick dark eyes, wit-lines at the corners; dark suit, plain white pointed collar, no ornament — the argument is the ornament.

**Image prompt — headshot:** Bust-length view, cropped just below the collarbone, of a compact white woman in her late fifties with a narrow, quick face and a sharp, long nose. Expressive, thick dark eyebrows; bright dark eyes with deep smile lines at the corners; a small wry mouth; silver-streaked dark curly hair pinned back with a pair of reading glasses pushed up into it and forgotten. Alert, amused, formidable expression, facing the viewer. At the bottom edge of the frame only: a plain white pointed blouse collar over a dark suit, no jewelry.

**Image prompt — full body & wardrobe:** Full-figure view of a compact white woman in her late fifties standing straight with a thick bound legal volume cradled in one arm. She wears a dark, well-cut skirt suit with a plain white pointed-collar blouse, sensible dark heels, and a second pair of reading glasses on a cord at her chest.

### George Oyama — Records
**Concept:** The agency's archivist and document man. Spent 1942–45 behind wire at Heart Mountain as a kid and came out with a cold, permanent understanding of what paperwork does to people — so he became the man paperwork cannot fool.

**Motivation (specific):** Documents put his family behind a fence; documents can put a machine behind bars. He treats every file as a witness that hasn't been questioned properly yet. The Vera case is personal in a way he will never once mention: a city disappearing people through routing stamps is the mechanism he has been waiting his whole adult life to catch in the act.

**Performable mannerisms:** Reads documents upside down across desks, fluently, out of habit. Handles paper by the edges. Dates everything, including his lunch bag. When a document is lying, he gets quieter, not louder — the room learns to watch for it.

**Voice:**
- [on the docket]: "Routing stamps are a handwriting. This one's ambidextrous. That's not a compliment."
- [method]: "Paper doesn't lie. People lie, and then they make paper, and the paper remembers what order they lied in."
- [the once, quietly]: "I've seen a government file a family into a horse stall. Took four forms. Whoever built this used five. Improvement, I suppose."

**Visual description:** Japanese-American man, early forties, a calm full face — deliberately unlike Sato's angularity — with a premature gray streak at the front of neat black hair. No glasses; heavy-lidded, patient eyes. Cardigan over shirt and a small neat bow tie; a permanent writer's callus and one ink-darkened middle finger.

**Image prompt — headshot:** Bust-length view, cropped just below the collarbone, of a Japanese-American man in his early forties with a calm, full, rounded face and a smooth high forehead. Neat, medium dark eyebrows; patient, heavy-lidded dark eyes with no glasses; a soft straight nose; a small composed mouth; neat short black hair with a distinct premature gray streak at the front. Quiet, thorough, unhurried expression, facing the viewer. At the bottom edge of the frame only: a small neat bow tie on a white collar under a buttoned cardigan.

**Image prompt — full body & wardrobe:** Full-figure view of a Japanese-American man in his early forties standing straight with a single manila folder held flat in both hands by its edges. He wears a buttoned wool cardigan over a white shirt and small neat bow tie, pressed gray trousers, and polished plain shoes.
